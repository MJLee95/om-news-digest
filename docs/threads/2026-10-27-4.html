Mexico 2026 national trucking strike over cargo theft and toll credits

Last updated Oct 28, 2026 · first opened Oct 28, 2026

CANACAR and other Mexican trucking groups began an indefinite nationwide strike on Oct 26, 2026 over cargo theft, extortion, road conditions, operating costs and a narrower toll-related tax incentive in the 2027 revenue law. On Oct 27 the Interior Ministry (Segob) and CANACAR, HAMOTAC and FEMATRAC agreed to four rounds of high-level talks, and blockades were reported lifted. The tax and security outcomes are still open.

Background

Around Oct 22, 2026, the trucking group AMOTAC blocked several Puebla highways for more than eight hours after one of its regional delegates was murdered. On Oct 23, CANACAR president Augusto Ramos Melo announced an indefinite national strike starting Oct 26, using the slogan "We don't want to stop Mexico. We want Mexico to hear us." On Oct 25 CANACAR confirmed plans for 19 states. Segob said most trucking groups had agreed to talk and that it was surprised by CANACAR's call. The same day, La Jornada reported that Hacienda (the Finance Ministry) proposed lowering the eligibility cap for the truckers' tax deduction from 300 million to 250 million pesos. The strike began about 6:30 a.m. on Oct 26. Actions included blockades on the México–Puebla, Puebla–Orizaba and Cuacnopalan–Oaxaca highways and an indefinite engine stop in Manzanillo, with trucks parked in company yards.

Why it is happening

Three sets of grievances drive the strike. (1) Security: cargo theft, violent highway robbery and extortion by criminal groups. (2) Costs: poor road conditions, excessive tow-truck charges and toll prices. (3) Tax: a cut to the incentive truckers receive under the 2027 federal revenue law (Ley de Ingresos), reported as the narrower 300M→250M peso eligibility cap. FreightWaves frames this as changes to a toll tax credit. Infobae also cites recent large-scale US visa losses by Mexican cargo drivers as background, but no link to the strike was confirmed.

Where things stand

Blockades, some lasting up to 34 hours, were reported lifted after the Oct 27 deal. Four high-level sessions were agreed, covering highway security, infrastructure, tax incentives, regulation and operating costs. The strike's size is disputed: Segob counted 38 protest sites in 19 states with 691 vehicles and 858 participants, while CANACAR claims protests reached all 32 states. Still open: the dates and outcomes of the sessions, the final treatment of the incentive in the 2027 revenue law, whether CANACAR has formally ended the strike or only the blockades, and the effect at the US–Mexico border.

What to watch

  • Scheduling and outcomes of the four Segob–trucker negotiating sessions
  • Final congressional passage of the 2027 Ley de Ingresos and whether the 300M→250M peso cap survives
  • Any renewed CANACAR or AMOTAC blockades if talks stall
  • Reported data on border crossing delays or cargo-theft incidence after the strike
