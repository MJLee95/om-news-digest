California SB 54 Packaging EPR: Baseline Revisions and Delayed 2027 Target

Last updated Nov 25, 2026 · first opened Nov 25, 2026

Putting California's SB 54 packaging extended producer responsibility law into practice is running into two problems. CalRecycle's provisional 2023 plastic baseline rests on data from only about half of producers and is expected to rise. Circular Action Alliance, the producer responsibility organization, says the first 10% source-reduction target (Jan. 1, 2027) will be missed and is seeking exemptions that would push it to 2030.

Background

SB 54 (Chapter 75, Statutes of 2022) took effect in 2022. It covers single-use packaging and plastic food serviceware and shifts end-of-life costs to producers. It requires a 25% cut in single-use plastic packaging and food serviceware by 2032. As reported by Packaging Dive, the interim targets are 10% by Jan. 1, 2027 (at least 2% via reuse/refill) and 20% by 2030; the statutory interim figures still need checking against PRC §42057. The law also creates a $500M-per-year, 10-year Plastic Pollution Mitigation Fund and restricts expanded polystyrene foodservice ware unless set recycling rates are met. Key steps so far: (1) On Dec. 31, 2024, CalRecycle's first Source Reduction Baseline Report estimated 2023 volumes from models: 2.9M tons and 171.4B components. (2) Revised proposed regulations were published in the Regulatory Notice Register in 2025 (Vol. 34-Z). (3) OAL approved the permanent rules, which took effect May 1, 2026. (4) Producers had to register by June 1, 2026, and file baseline reports on 2023 data by July 1, 2026. (5) CAA submitted its program plan by June 15, 2026, and a revised plan on Oct. 13, 2026. (6) In early November 2026, CalRecycle updated the baseline with producer-reported data: 1.03M tons and 222B components, from 2,127 producers.

Why it is happening

First, the baseline is incomplete. Only about half of producers reported, while CAA says more than 4,000 are now registered. CalRecycle says it will not leave the baseline at a number that does not represent all participant producers. Second, the regulatory timeline was compressed. CAA argues the final rules came too late and its plan is still unapproved, and both happened after the 2026 measurement period had begun, so it could not organize compliance for the 2027 target. Third, the statute provides a relief valve: exemptions of up to five years for material-specific or related unique challenges. Finally, if the baseline is corrected upward, more absolute tonnage must be cut to reach each percentage target.

Where things stand

The regulations have been in effect since May 1, 2026. CAA's revised program plan (submitted Oct. 13, 2026) awaits CalRecycle approval. The baseline of 1.03M tons and 222B components is provisional and expected to rise. On Nov. 23, 2026, CAA's draft program plan included unique challenge exemption applications. These would meet the 10% 2027 target in 2030, based on 2029 producer supply. CAA says it is too early to know whether the 2030 or 2032 targets will need exemptions. CalRecycle has not publicly decided on the exemptions or the plan. It is unclear how a later baseline correction will interact with measuring the 2027 target.

What to watch

  • CalRecycle decision on CAA's revised program plan and its unique challenge exemption requests (date not confirmed)
  • Jan. 1, 2027: statutory 10% source-reduction target (at least 2% via reuse/refill)
  • July 2027: deadline for non-reporting producers to submit 2023 baseline data
  • November 2027: corrected (expected higher) source-reduction baseline from CalRecycle
