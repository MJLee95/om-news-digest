Senate permitting bill S. 5653 and data center grid costs

Last updated Nov 8, 2026 · first opened Nov 8, 2026

S. 5653, the bipartisan permitting package introduced Sept. 30, 2026, would make large computing loads pay more of the cost of transmission. A Grid Strategies estimate puts that at about $55 billion over 10 years. A reported final manager's amendment keeps a 'large loads pay' rule for 20 MW+ loads ahead of a 60-vote cloture vote. That vote's outcome is unknown.

Background

Sen. Shelley Moore Capito introduced S. 5653, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, on Sept. 30, 2026, with Sens. Lee, Whitehouse and Heinrich. It was referred to Senate Energy and Natural Resources.

The bill has two divisions:

  • Division A: NEPA, Clean Water Act and ESA changes, plus 'project certainty' limits on revoking issued permits.
  • Division B: transmission sections covering permitting (§2101), planning (§2103), an end to utilities' right of first refusal (§2105), 'ratepayer protection' (§2107), interconnection queue reform (§2110) and data collection on 'computational load entities' (§2114).

On Nov. 2, 2026, TechTarget reported a Grid Strategies estimate that the bill would shift about $55 billion in transmission costs to data centers over 10 years. Around Saturday, Nov. 7, senators reportedly released a final manager's amendment, with a cloture vote set for the following Monday (likely Nov. 9). That report is unconfirmed.

Why it is happening

Data-center demand growth is concentrated in PJM and MISO. It is raising grid investment needs and consumer power bills, which has made 'make data centers pay' a bipartisan affordability message.

The package appears to be a cross-committee deal. Republicans mostly want faster NEPA reviews and fewer lawsuits. Democrats mostly want more transmission and protection for ratepayers. This characterization is the researcher's inference.

States want to keep control over line siting and retail rates. That explains the reported one-year state-first window before FERC's backstop authority applies, and the §2107(b) 'Ratepayer Protection Floor' requiring states to address the generation costs large computing loads create.

Where things stand

The bill was introduced and referred to committee. A manager's amendment was reportedly released around Nov. 7, 2026. Reported provisions include:

  • a one-year state-review requirement for 345 kV+ lines before FERC's backstop applies
  • retention of the rule that new loads of 20 MW or more pay the full cost of serving them
  • a new DOE Data Center Community Assistance Program

None of these details are verified. The result of the cloture vote (needs 60) is unknown. The $55B figure comes from an advocacy consultancy, not CBO or FERC. About 82% of the cost falls in PJM and MISO, and ERCOT is excluded. The estimate assumes new data centers buy firm transmission service.

What to watch

  • Cloture vote result on S. 5653, around Nov. 9, 2026 (60 votes needed), and any final passage vote
  • Text of the manager's amendment on congress.gov, especially §2107 cost allocation and §2114 computational-load data collection
  • Reconciliation with the House NEPA-focused permitting bill (SPEED Act), if the Senate passes S. 5653
  • FERC rulemaking to implement large-load transmission pricing, if enacted
