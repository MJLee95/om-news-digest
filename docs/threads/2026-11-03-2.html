Senate permitting bill S. 5653 (BAAJA) and data center grid costs

Last updated Nov 20, 2026 · first opened Nov 8, 2026

S. 5653, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, would speed federal permitting, expand FERC's role in interstate transmission, and make 20 MW+ computational loads pay the full incremental cost of serving them. It passed the Senate 62–33 on Nov. 19, 2026, and now goes to a House where passage is uncertain before Democrats take control in January 2027.

Background

Background, from the researcher's own knowledge and not checked: Manchin and Barrasso's Energy Permitting Reform Act of 2024 cleared the Senate energy committee in July 2024 but died. The House passed Rep. Westerman's NEPA-focused SPEED Act in December 2025. Both stalled.

Also in December 2025, Trump halted five offshore wind projects. Sens. Whitehouse and Heinrich then called permitting reform "dead in the water." Courts later ruled against the administration.

A deal came together in late September 2026. Utility Dive, citing Reuters, says it followed Trump assurances to Democrats on fair treatment for wind and solar. The bill lets developers sue over "disparate" federal treatment by project type.

Timeline:

  • Sept. 30, 2026: Sens. Capito, Whitehouse, Lee and Heinrich introduced S. 5653.
  • Nov. 2: TechTarget reported a roughly $55B, 10-year transmission cost shift to data centers.
  • Nov. 7: the sponsors released a substitute (manager's) amendment.
  • Nov. 9: Senate voted 73–25 to begin debate.
  • Nov. 16: Senate voted 70–27 to proceed on the substitute.
  • Nov. 18: Senate voted 65–31 to end debate (cloture).
  • Nov. 19: Senate passed the bill 62–33.

Why it is happening

AI-driven electricity demand is rising fast, and household power bills are going up. That has made "make data centers pay" a bipartisan affordability message, and the bill's sponsors frame it as holding big tech to its pledge not to shift costs onto other customers.

Energy projects of all types have long been slowed by permitting and litigation. Fossil and clean-energy developers both want faster, more predictable approvals.

The resulting trade:

  • Republicans mainly get NEPA, Clean Water Act, ESA and NHPA streamlining and "project certainty."
  • Democrats mainly get transmission reform, protection for wind and solar, and ratepayer safeguards.

States' interest in keeping control of siting and retail rates explains the state-first window before FERC's backstop siting authority applies.

Where things stand

Passed the Senate 62–33 on Nov. 19, 2026. It passed on the Nov. 7 substitute text only; changes negotiated in the final days were left out.

Data-center provisions:

  • Loads of 20 MW or more pay the full incremental cost of serving them (reportedly §2107). That cost cannot be shifted to other customers.
  • Large loads must post financial guarantees, pay exit fees, and remain liable for stranded costs if they leave.

Other reported provisions:

  • FERC backstop permitting of interstate lines after a year of state review. Enverus says the introduced bill already allowed this after a year of state inaction, so how much the Nov. 7 text changed it (MLQ reports a 345 kV+ focus) is uncertain.
  • A DOE Data Center Community Assistance Program and 20-year joint planning of transmission and generation. These come from search snippets only and are unverified.

The $55B (TechTarget) and $50B (explainx) cost-shift estimates have no verified author or assumptions.

Open questions:

  • The House has not set a schedule or stated a position. Supporters had wanted 70–75 Senate votes to make House passage safe; the bill got 62.
  • Per Axios, any final version must clear both chambers before the end of the year.

What to watch

  • House leadership's decision before the end of 2026: amend the bill (sending it back to the Senate) or hold an up-or-down vote
  • Whether House Democrats push to delay action to January 2027, when Democrats control both chambers
  • Presidential signature if Congress clears the bill
  • FERC and state implementation of the 20 MW+ large-load cost rule and the state-first backstop siting process, if enacted

Earlier versions of this brief

Brief as of Nov 8, 2026: Senate permitting bill S. 5653 and data center grid costs

S. 5653, the bipartisan permitting package introduced Sept. 30, 2026, would make large computing loads pay more of the cost of transmission. A Grid Strategies estimate puts that at about $55 billion over 10 years. A reported final manager's amendment keeps a 'large loads pay' rule for 20 MW+ loads ahead of a 60-vote cloture vote. That vote's outcome is unknown.

Status then: The bill was introduced and referred to committee. A manager's amendment was reportedly released around Nov. 7, 2026. Reported provisions include: - a one-year state-review requirement for 345 kV+ lines before FERC's backstop applies - retention of the rule that new loads of 20 MW or more pay the full cost of serving them - a new DOE Data Center Community Assistance Program None of these details are verified. The result of the cloture vote (needs 60) is unknown. The $55B figure comes from an advocacy consultancy, not CBO or FERC. About 82% of the cost falls in PJM and MISO, and ERCOT is excluded. The estimate assumes new data centers buy firm transmission service.
