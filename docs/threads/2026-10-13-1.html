PJM data center load, curtailment rules and resource adequacy

Last updated Oct 19, 2026 · first opened Oct 19, 2026

Data center demand is growing faster than new generation and transmission in PJM. On Oct. 9, 2026 FERC rejected most of PJM's large-load plan, including the 'connect now, curtail first' service (IRAS). That leaves curtailment decisions to states such as Pennsylvania, while October Maximum Generation alerts showed how tight the grid already is.

Background

PJM's Dec. 2025 capacity auction for 2027/28 cleared at the $333.44/MW-day cap and came in 6,623 MW short of the reliability requirement. Nearly 5,100 MW of the 5,250 MW load growth behind that shortfall came from data centers.

The responses that followed:

  • Jan. 2026: the White House National Energy Dominance Council and all 13 PJM governors issued a Statement of Principles. It called for a backstop auction with 15-year price certainty, with costs charged to data centers that neither bring new capacity nor accept curtailment.
  • Jan. 16, 2026: PJM's Board set a 50 MW large-load definition. It offered new loads two paths: bring your own new generation, or 'connect and manage' with earlier curtailment.
  • March 2026: the White House 'Ratepayer Protection Pledge' asked large loads to 'build, bring, or buy' the generation they need.
  • July 14, 2026: the 2028/29 auction cleared at its $325/MW-day cap, 6,831 MW short.
  • July 31, 2026: PJM filed the backstop auction at FERC (ER26-3380).
  • Aug. 13, 2026: PJM filed the large-load package (ER26-3515): definition, registry, IRAS, a bring-your-own-capacity option and a demand-curve change.
  • Sept. 10, 2026: the Pennsylvania PUC voted 5-0 to rewrite its emergency load-control rules.
  • Sept. 29, 2026: FERC accepted the backstop auction but suspended it until Feb. 28, 2027, citing cost allocation, the rules for a utility leaving PJM, and collateral.

Why it is happening

  • Demand outpacing supply: year-round data center load is growing faster than new supply and transmission. Capacity auctions now clear at price caps and still fall short of the reliability requirement.
  • Seasonal squeeze: weather extremes now last longer and push into the spring and fall maintenance seasons.
  • Split jurisdiction: FERC regulates wholesale markets, while states control retail customers and rates. That division blocks a single PJM-wide rule on who gets curtailed and who pays.
  • Pressure to protect existing ratepayers: policymakers want new large loads to pay for the capacity they require rather than spreading the cost to other customers.

Where things stand

  • IRAS: dead as filed. Only the large-load definition and the registry (location, ramp schedule and capacity supply of each facility) survive.
  • December 2026 auction for 2029/30: the demand-curve change for IRAS loads will not apply.
  • Backstop auction: suspended until Feb. 28, 2027 unless PJM refiles and FERC acts sooner. PJM's bilateral matchmaking process continues.
  • Pennsylvania: taking comments on its Tentative Order (30 days plus 15 for replies); a final order is expected Jan. 28, 2027.
  • Other states: Jefferies expects any revival of curtailment rules to be state-led, with Ohio and Pennsylvania the ones to watch.
  • October operations: during the week of Oct. 12, 2026, just over 61,000 MW of generation was out (66% of fleet capacity available), about 15,000 MW of it unplanned (preliminary).
  • Demand response: PJM called it on Oct. 13–14. It has now been used on 15 days in 2025–2026, versus 8 in all of 2015–2024.
  • Open questions: whether and how PJM refiles either proposal.

What to watch

  • PJM's December 2026 capacity auction for 2029/30
  • Pennsylvania PUC final order on curtailing large loads, expected at the Jan. 28, 2027 public meeting
  • FERC backstop auction suspension ends Feb. 28, 2027, or earlier if PJM refiles
  • Whether Ohio or other PJM states adopt curtailment-first rules for large loads, and whether PJM refiles IRAS
