Cyberattacks on European supermarket refrigeration controls

Last updated Nov 3, 2026 · first opened Nov 3, 2026

Overnight cyberattacks on internet-connected store refrigeration controllers forced food disposal at about 700 French supermarkets on 12 Oct 2026 (Danfoss systems, mostly out-of-support models). A second wave on 1–2 Nov 2026 hit stores in Belgium, Luxembourg and Sweden. Attribution, method and whether the two waves are linked remain unconfirmed.

Background

In August 2026 a Claroty researcher reportedly found several flaws in Danfoss refrigeration controllers. This is unverified, and it is unknown whether those flaws were used. On the night of 11–12 Oct 2026, attackers reportedly made defrost cycles run non-stop and set some units to 50°C for hours in French supermarkets. Early counts were about 300 Intermarché/Netto stores and nearly 100 Coopérative U stores. On 13 Oct France's finance minister put the total at about 700 of roughly 10,000 supermarkets, including Coopérative U (80 of 2,000 stores), Carrefour and Monoprix. The Paris prosecutor's cybercrime unit opened an investigation into food losses and possible fires. ANSSI, France's national cybersecurity agency, reportedly identified the software and began coordinating with other European agencies. On 12 Oct Ahold Delhaize's Albert Heijn took its online systems offline after a 'security issue,' but AP did not link this to the fridge attacks.

Why it is happening

The targets were internet-connected refrigeration controllers. Danfoss said most affected units were older products no longer receiving security updates. That points to legacy, unpatched operational-technology (OT) equipment left exposed to the internet, but no official technical analysis confirms this. Who did it and why is unknown: there is no claim of responsibility, no attribution and no ransom demand. Underneath, retailers depend on long-lived cold-chain equipment whose service life outlasts vendor security support.

Where things stand

The French criminal investigation is open. The second wave in Belgium, Luxembourg and Sweden has no official statements yet from those countries' authorities. Still unconfirmed: attribution, arrests, a full Danfoss advisory, the causes of the reported fires, and whether the second wave used the same equipment and method.

What to watch

  • Danfoss security advisories and any CISA or CERT-FR industrial-control advisories on its refrigeration controllers
  • Paris prosecutor and ANSSI updates on attribution and method
  • Statements from Belgium's CCB, Sweden's national cyber agency and the affected retailers on whether the Nov wave involved the same controllers
  • Retailer loss disclosures and insurance claims in upcoming quarterly reports (e.g., Ahold Delhaize, Carrefour)
