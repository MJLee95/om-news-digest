ILWU Local 26 watchmen contract dispute at LA–Long Beach

Last updated Nov 22, 2026 · first opened Nov 22, 2026

About 250 ILWU Local 26 port watchmen (security guards) threatened to strike at six LA–Long Beach container terminals that, per the Pacific Maritime Association (PMA), handle more than half of the two ports' combined cargo. A federally mediated tentative agreement reached just before the 12:01 a.m. Nov. 21, 2026 deadline averted the strike. Terms are undisclosed and ratification is pending.

Background

Bargaining ran for more than a year, under federal mediation for at least part of that time, so it likely began in 2025 or earlier. The exact start date is not confirmed. Local 26 members authorized a strike in late October 2026, and talks were then extended three times. On Nov. 17, 2026, PMA said talks had ended with 'little to no movement from the union' and that it would not extend past 12:01 a.m. Saturday, Nov. 21. A tentative deal was reached just before that deadline.

Why it is happening

The specific issues in dispute (pay, staffing, technology, benefits) have not been made public. The union's leverage comes from concentration. The watchmen are only about 2% of the ILWU workforce at the two ports, but they work at six terminals that PMA says handle about 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% of Los Angeles cargo. That makes a small unit a potential chokepoint. The timing also mattered: Sourcing Journal tied it to holiday restocking that was already delayed by rail congestion.

Where things stand

A strike was averted by a tentative agreement reached under federal mediation just before the 12:01 a.m. Nov. 21, 2026 deadline. Final terms are undisclosed. Members must still ratify, and the existing contract remains in force until the vote. Still open: the ratification date and outcome, the substance of the disputed issues, and the vote tallies.
