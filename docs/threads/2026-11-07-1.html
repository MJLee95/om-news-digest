Texas data-center grid audit, permit freeze and political transition

Last updated Nov 27, 2026 · first opened Nov 27, 2026

Texas paused ERCOT grid connections for data centers of 75 MW or more in August 2026 while it audits the large-load queue, and it froze TCEQ environmental permits from Sept. 21. The TCEQ freeze lifted in mid-November. The ERCOT connection hold appears to remain in place pending a Dec. 10 report, and Governor-elect Hinojosa has pledged a new freeze when she takes office Jan. 19, 2027.

Background

In June 2025 Gov. Abbott signed SB 6, which set new interconnection rules for loads of 75 MW or more; this is the source of the 75 MW threshold. ERCOT was flooded with requests and created 'Batch Zero', a group study that allocates capacity among large loads. By mid-2026 ERCOT reported about 474.7 GW of large-load requests, roughly 90% of them data centers. On Aug. 3, 2026 Abbott ordered the PUC (Chair Thomas Gleeson) and ERCOT (CEO Pablo Vegas) to verify and audit every data center in the queue and to deny connection to projects that fail. ERCOT paused Batch Zero the same day. In mid-August ERCOT said the audit covers about 250–300 projects and stopped approving new connections of 75 MW or more. Projects of 25–75 MW were reportedly not paused, and 17 loads (about 6.6 GW) that had cleared every other step were left stranded. On Sept. 14, ERCOT notice M-B091426-01 asked data centers of 25 MW or more for community-impact information, due Oct. 12. On Sept. 21 Abbott directed TCEQ to halt data-center permits until the audits finished. On Nov. 3, Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the governorship according to unofficial results.

Why it is happening

The queue is far larger than what will be built. Requests total about 474 GW, more than five times ERCOT's record peak demand, while BNEF expects only about 17 GW of installed Texas data-center capacity by 2030. Developers file speculative and duplicate requests. Public backlash over grid reliability, water use and local impacts adds pressure; Lubbock, for example, has proposed a 90-day moratorium. Abbott cited data centers that did not answer a PUC survey on water and power use. The election of a governor who pledges tighter limits adds political urgency.

Where things stand

TCEQ resumed normal permit review around Nov. 13–16 after the governor's office said ERCOT had finished collecting community-impact data; for example, an air permit was issued for SpaceX's Starbase computing project. ERCOT's hold on connections of 75 MW or more still appeared to be in place in late November. The '1,800+ projects resumed' headline conflates ERCOT's whole queue with the TCEQ permit restart. A reported ERCOT probe into developers downsizing projects to stay under 75 MW could not be verified; it is plausible given the threshold design, but unconfirmed. Several facts rest on a tracker site rather than primary documents.
