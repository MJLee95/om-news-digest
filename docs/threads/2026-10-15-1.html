California SB 253/SB 261 climate disclosure rollout and challenges

Last updated Oct 31, 2026 · first opened Oct 31, 2026

California's corporate climate-disclosure regime is taking effect. CARB's initial regulation was approved Oct 12, 2026, and the first SB 253 Scope 1 and 2 emissions reports are due Nov 10, 2026. Meanwhile, SB 261 remains enjoined pending a Ninth Circuit merits ruling, and the OCC is pressing CARB to exempt national banks.

Background

SB 253 (Scope 1–3 GHG reporting for firms over $1B in revenue doing business in California) and SB 261 (biennial climate-risk reports for firms over $500M) passed in 2023. The US Chamber of Commerce and co-plaintiffs challenged both laws in federal court. In Aug 2025 the district court denied a preliminary injunction. The plaintiffs then sought an injunction pending appeal from the Ninth Circuit in Sept 2025. On Nov 18, 2025, the Ninth Circuit enjoined SB 261 only, effectively suspending its Jan 1, 2026 deadline, and let SB 253 proceed. In Dec 2025 CARB said it would not enforce the SB 261 deadline and opened a voluntary public docket for SB 261 reports. Oral argument followed on Jan 9, 2026. CARB adopted its Initial Regulation on Feb 26, 2026, setting annual fees of about $3,106 per company under SB 253 and $1,403 under SB 261. It submitted the rule to OAL in May, withdrew it in June (which moved the first deadline from Aug 10 to Nov 10), and resubmitted in Sept. OAL approved the rule on Oct 12, 2026 (17 CCR §§96070–96077).

Why it is happening

California is using state law to force emissions and climate-risk disclosure from large public and private companies, filling a gap in federal mandates. Opponents argue the laws compel speech on the state's preferred climate views (First Amendment). The litigation also raises Commerce Clause, Supremacy Clause and Clean Air Act claims, and a separate challenge argues SB 261 is preempted by federal securities law. The federal bank regulator objects that applying the laws to federally chartered banks conflicts with federal supervision and safety-and-soundness rules and burdens smaller banks. CARB has softened year one to keep the timeline credible: it accepts already-collected data, uses consolidated parent reports, and, per ESG Today, does not require assurance in year one.

Where things stand

SB 253 is in force. Scope 1 and 2 reports are due Nov 10, 2026, with first-year enforcement flexibility, use of existing data and consolidated parent reports. Scope 3 begins in 2027. SB 261 remains enjoined, with no new deadline set; voluntary reports can be posted to CARB's docket. The Ninth Circuit merits ruling is pending and could come at any time. The OCC has sent only a letter, not a formal preemption. It is unknown whether CARB will respond or grant a bank exemption, and analysts advise banks not to stop preparing. Coverage counts are inconsistent: ESG Today cites more than 4,000 companies, while Watershed estimated about 2,600 for SB 253 and about 4,100 for SB 261.

What to watch

  • Nov 10, 2026: first SB 253 Scope 1 and 2 reporting deadline; watch filing volume and quality on CARB's systems
  • Ninth Circuit merits decision in Chamber of Commerce v. Sanchez (timing unknown), which could revive SB 261 or enjoin SB 253
  • CARB response to the OCC's Oct 29 letter, and any formal OCC preemption action
  • CARB's 2027 rulemaking: annual Nov 10 deadline, Scope 3 phase-in for five categories, assurance standards, and a fixed GHG Protocol version
