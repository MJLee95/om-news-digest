01 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Pennsylvania PUC proposes curtailing new data centers before other customers during grid emergencies

NorthcentralPA.com · Oct 17 · Read · ☆ Star
↳ Follow-up to Oct 13 · FERC rejects PJM's curtailment-based large-load interconnection plan, approves d, Oct 17 · PJM ran on 66% of its generation fleet during October Maximum Generation Alerts;

On Oct. 1, 2026 the Pennsylvania PUC voted 4-0 for a Tentative Order. Under it, utilities would curtail eligible new large loads, such as data centers that haven't secured enough new capacity, before other customers during PJM 'Interim Resource Adequacy Service' events, and could not classify data centers as exempt 'critical load.' Utilities would also have to keep large-load registries and set notification procedures. A PUC-commissioned study found PJM failing its reliability planning standard from 2027 to 2030, with modeled risk nearly six times the standard by 2030. Comments are due within 30 days plus 15 for replies, and CBS reports a final order is expected at the end of January.

OM angleThis turns large-load interruptibility into a regulated priority rule, which changes the expected cost of siting a data center in one PJM state versus its neighbors. It is a clean setting for studying curtailment priority, capacity procurement and facility location together.
Research QDoes a state rule placing un-backed large loads first in the curtailment queue shift data-center siting, or the speed of bring-your-own-capacity deals, away from Pennsylvania toward other PJM states?
Data / IDTreat Pennsylvania as treated and other PJM states as controls, with the Oct. 2026 Tentative Order and the expected January final order as shock dates. Measure outcomes with large-load interconnection requests and utility large-load filings in state PUC dockets, PJM load forecasts, and new-generation and co-location filings in EIA-860 and LBNL queue data. The unit of analysis is the utility zone by quarter.
Keywordsinterruptible load · data center electricity demand · grid reliability standards · facility siting · federal-state jurisdiction

02 · ESG & Disclosure

Mighty Earth report: US firms held 350+ EU meetings to delay and weaken the Deforestation Regulation

The Guardian · Oct 17 · Read · ☆ Star

The Guardian (Oct. 17, 2026) reported a Mighty Earth investigation finding that 24 US companies and trade groups held more than 350 meetings with EU officials between July 2024 and July 2026 over the EUDR: 173 with MEPs and 178 with senior Commission officials. These figures are relayed second-hand. Named players include Cargill, Bunge, Mondelēz, the US Soybean Export Council and AmCham EU. After a July 2025 Trump–von der Leyen meeting, a joint statement promised the US special treatment, and the Commission called the US 'negligible risk.' The firms deny coordinating and cite implementation problems such as IT systems and unclear obligations.

OM angleTraceability and due-diligence costs in commodity supply chains depend on how the rules end up written. Lobbying intensity and country-risk designations are measurable inputs that shape those rules.
Research QDo commodity firms with greater exposure to EUDR-covered sourcing lobby more intensively, and did 'negligible risk' country designations shift EU sourcing of soy and wood products toward low-risk-rated origins?
Data / IDFirm-level meeting counts would come from the EU Transparency Register and Commission meeting logs. EUDR exposure could be built from commodity import shares in UN Comtrade (Panjiva bills of lading where available). A difference-in-differences design would compare EU imports by origin before and after the July 2025 US designation and the later postponement and dilution steps, whose legal status should be verified.
Keywordsdeforestation · multi-tier traceability · regulatory uncertainty · corporate lobbying
Related research

03 · Supply Chain Resilience

Infineon adds Wolfspeed 200mm silicon carbide wafers in multi-year supply deal

ELE Times · Oct 17 · Read · ☆ Star

On Oct. 15, 2026 Infineon and Wolfspeed announced a multi-year agreement for Wolfspeed to supply 200mm SiC wafers. Wolfspeed calls it an expansion and extension of their existing 150mm deal, a relationship of more than 20 years, while Infineon frames it as supporting its move from 150mm to 200mm. Infineon cites its multi-supplier strategy and rising SiC demand from EVs, renewables and AI data center power systems. Value, volumes and duration were not disclosed.

OM angleThis is a long-term contract signed during a technology transition, with the buyer deliberately multi-sourcing a concentrated input. It is useful for studying how wafer-size transitions interact with supplier diversification and the risk of a supplier failing.
Research QWhen a critical input undergoes a technology node transition (150mm to 200mm SiC), do buyers lengthen contracts and add suppliers, and how does supplier financial distress affect those choices?
Data / IDCollect supply-agreement disclosures from SEC EDGAR 8-Ks and 10-K material-contract exhibits, plus Compustat customer segments, to build buyer–supplier dyads. FactSet Revere is a commercial alternative. The design would compare contract terms and the number of suppliers per buyer before and after 200mm announcements across SiC buyers, conditioning on supplier distress measures from Compustat.
Keywordssupplier diversification · supplier concentration · long-term supply contracts · silicon carbide
Related research
Procurement Strategies with Unreliable Suppliers Under Correlated Random Yields (Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 2021)
Analyzes procurement from multiple unreliable suppliers with correlated yields, directly relevant to multi-sourcing a technically demanding wafer input.
Wholesale Price Contracts for Reliable Supply (Production and Operations Management, 2018)
Shows when simple wholesale contracts can induce reliable supply, a benchmark for long-term wafer agreements with undisclosed terms.
04 · Circular Economy

India's draft National Steel Policy 2047 targets 604 MT and emphasizes circularity and secondary producers

ET Manufacturing · Oct 17 · Read · ☆ Star

PTI reported on Oct. 17 that industry welcomed India's draft National Steel Policy 2047, released for comment. Its 2047 targets are 604 MT of crude steel (sources disagree on whether this is capacity or production), 505 MT of finished-steel demand, 302 kg per capita consumption and 45 MT of exports. The draft stresses value addition, raw-material security, decarbonisation, coated and specialty steel, and support for secondary producers. RHI Magnesita India highlighted the draft's recognition of refractory recovery.

OM angleThis is a large policy shift in the world's fastest-growing major steel market, linking circularity (secondary steel, refractory recovery) with raw-material security and decarbonisation. It is relevant to recycling capacity and secondary-material sourcing.
Research QHow do policy commitments to secondary steel producers and material recovery change the mix of primary and secondary steelmaking capacity, and India's import dependence for scrap and raw materials?
Data / IDTrack India's imports of ferrous scrap, coking coal and magnesite by HS code in UN Comtrade before and after the draft and the final policy, with peer steel-producing countries as comparison. Pair this with plant-level capacity and production statistics. Note that the final policy text and timing still need to be verified.
Keywordssteel decarbonization · secondary materials · raw material security · industrial policy
05 · Critical Minerals & Batteries

Pentagon commits $450M to tungsten producer Elmet in exchange for preferred equity and warrants

Stocktwits · Oct 17 · Read · ☆ Star

On Sept. 14, 2026 Elmet Group announced a $450M committed investment from the US Department of War to expand tungsten mining, processing and manufacturing: $200M at closing, with further drawdowns. In return the government gets redeemable preferred equity, warrants for up to 19.9% of common stock, and a board seat plus an observer. The Department also took about a 10% stake through roughly $35.6M of transactions involving Trilogy Metals and South32's Alaska projects. This is a late-September wrap; ELMT rose over 33% that week, and the deal's closing and the subsequent Trump–Xi meeting outcome are unconfirmed here.

OM angleGovernments are moving from grants and loans to equity and governance rights in mineral suppliers. That raises questions about how state ownership changes capacity investment and sourcing for concentrated inputs like tungsten.
Research QHow do markets and firms respond when the US government takes equity stakes in critical-mineral producers? Specifically, do recipients accelerate capacity investment, and do rivals' valuations move?
Data / IDBuild an event study on announcement dates of government equity deals (Elmet, Trilogy and others), using 8-K exhibits from SEC EDGAR and CRSP returns for recipients and listed peers. Follow-up outcomes would be capex and capacity from Compustat and 10-Ks, and US import dependence for tungsten from USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries.
Keywordssupplier concentration · capacity investment under uncertainty · government equity stakes · tungsten

New in the journals

EXPRESS: Valuation Bottlenecks: Why Isn’t a Better Business Enough for a Higher Market Capitalization?
Production and Operations Management

The paper asks why operational or disclosure improvements often don't raise market value. It models equity value as the minimum across several valuation 'lenses,' so the single weakest firm condition (for example, a governance or financing concern) binds and gets disproportionate weight.

This helps interpret reactions like Elmet's jump after government backing resolved a financing constraint, or why ESG improvements may not be priced while another concern dominates.
