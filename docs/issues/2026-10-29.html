01 · Supply Chain Resilience

About 35,700 import containers wait for on-dock rail at LA–Long Beach amid record volumes and reported railcar shortages

FreightWaves · Oct 28 · Read · ☆ Star

On 26 Oct, 35,688 import containers were waiting for on-dock rail at the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach. Of these, 10,562 had been at the terminals for nine days or more. Los Angeles had 19,626 waiting (41.4% for nine days or more) and Long Beach had 16,062 (15.1%). Average rail dwell rose to 7.19 days in September from 6.75 in August, while local-truck dwell rose to 3.18 days from 2.95.

BackgroundBoth ports set monthly records in September: about 1.04M TEUs at Los Angeles and 997,177 TEUs at Long Beach. PMSA links the longer dwell times to an extended peak season. BNSF and Union Pacific serve the complex. C.H. Robinson's October update named Southern California intermodal railcar shortages as a constraint and said empty-container repositioning was complicating equipment flows, while truck-bound boxes moved fairly normally. Offshore, only one ship was waiting for a berth that week, so the queue has moved from water to land. Separately, PMA warned that about 250 ILWU Local 26 watchmen could strike on 1 Nov at eight terminals. Those terminals handle about 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% of Los Angeles cargo.
ProblemMarine-side capacity is keeping up, but the inland rail link is not, so containers pile up at terminals. That ties up yard space and delays inland deliveries. Shippers must choose between waiting for rail or switching to more expensive trucking or transloading. A possible strike adds a second, labor-driven shock on top of the capacity squeeze.
Where & whyThe problem sits at the port–rail interface, the on-dock rail handoff. It arises from a mismatch between railcar supply and import surges, and from equipment imbalances caused by empty-container repositioning. It is also a coordination problem: terminals, two Class I railroads and ocean carriers each control part of the flow, but no one optimizes the whole system. The two ports show sharply different shares of long-dwell boxes, which suggests terminal- or railroad-specific frictions. The railcar shortage is reported by C.H. Robinson and has not been confirmed by the railroads.
Stakeholders
BNSF and Union Pacific — Railcar and crew allocation across the network; reputational and regulatory exposure if port service lags
Port terminals (LA and Long Beach) — Yard congestion and lower throughput while boxes sit; possible strike disruption at eight terminals
Importers / beneficial cargo owners — Longer inland lead times, demurrage/detention costs, and the choice to shift to truck or transload
ILWU Local 26 and PMA — Wage and contract terms for watchmen; leverage from timing a strike during a congestion peak
Research hints
💡 The gap between the two ports (41.4% vs 15.1% of boxes waiting nine days or more) is a puzzle worth unpacking: terminal operator, railroad mix or carrier alliances?
💡 Queueing and network models of tandem stages (vessel → yard → rail) can show how a bottleneck migrates from berth to rail.
💡 Treat the possible 1 Nov strike as an event: do importers pre-shift cargo to truck or to other ports in anticipation?
Data leadsPort of LA/LB daily rail reports (as compiled by The Dwell), PMSA monthly dwell-time data, NOAA AIS vessel tracks for berth queues, and STB railroad service metrics; commercial bill-of-lading data (Panjiva) for shipper-level routing.
Keywordsport congestion · intermodal rail · labor disruption · capacity utilization · dwell time
Related research
Scheduled Service Network Design for Freight Rail Transportation (Operations Research, 2014)
Models freight rail service network design, including car blocking and train makeup; useful for thinking about how railcar allocation creates or relieves port backlogs.
Introducing synchromodality: One missing link between transportation and supply chain management (Journal of Supply Chain Management, 2021)
Develops the synchromodality construct, the flexible real-time switching between modes, which matches shippers' choice between waiting for rail or switching to truck.
02 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Mitsubishi Power says new gas turbine orders now face delivery around 2031

Energy News Beat · Oct 28 · Read · ☆ Star

Akihiro Ondo, CEO of Mitsubishi Power Asia Pacific, said at Singapore International Energy Week on 27 Oct that new gas turbine orders face delivery "at around 2031." He added that 2028–2029 slots are almost full. He projected this year's global orders at about 70–80 GW, well above average, with most demand from North America and growing interest from Southeast Asia, especially Malaysia and Singapore, driven by AI.

BackgroundParent company MHI said in 2025 it aimed to double production capacity within two years. Bloomberg reports that Mitsubishi, GE Vernova and Siemens Energy are all struggling to keep pace with demand from data centers, manufacturing and electrification. In August, MHI's CFO said orders booked that quarter were due for delivery in 2028–2030. Yahoo Finance separately reports that GE Vernova's heavy-duty turbine schedule also reaches 2031.
ProblemData center developers and utilities that want firm gas generation face lead times of five years or more. That pushes them to reserve slots early on uncertain demand forecasts, turn to other power sources, or delay projects. OEMs must decide how far to expand capacity without overbuilding if AI demand cools.
Where & whyThe bottleneck is upstream, in a concentrated equipment market with three main heavy-duty turbine makers. Production capacity is slow to expand and demand has surged. Lead times stretch because of supplier concentration, OEM caution about cyclical demand (a capacity investment under uncertainty problem), and buyers rushing to queue slots, which may inflate order books. Caveats: "around 2031" is a verbal estimate, the 70–80 GW is a projection, and no specific delayed projects were named.
Stakeholders
Turbine OEMs (Mitsubishi Power/MHI, GE Vernova, Siemens Energy) — Pricing power and backlog, against the risk of overexpanding capacity if demand falls
Data center developers — Time-to-power for new campuses; may shift to grid power, other generation or other sites
Utilities — Meeting load-growth and reliability obligations on time; cost recovery for expensive late capacity
Southeast Asian governments (e.g., Malaysia, Singapore) — Attracting AI investment without power supply constraints
Research hints
💡 The situation resembles the semiconductor equipment cycle: think about forecast inflation and phantom orders when buyers race for scarce slots.
💡 Capacity expansion under demand uncertainty in an oligopoly: why might OEMs rationally under-build?
💡 Compare turbine lead times with the timing of data center siting and interconnection to see whether equipment or the grid is the binding constraint.
Data leadsEIA-860 planned generator additions and their status changes, LBNL interconnection queue data for gas projects, and OEM backlog and order disclosures in earnings calls (MHI, GE Vernova and Siemens Energy filings).
Keywordssupplier concentration · capacity investment under uncertainty · data center electricity demand · pre-buy behavior · gas turbines
Related research
An Empirical Analysis of Forecast Sharing in the Semiconductor Equipment Supply Chain (Management Science, 2005)
Shows empirically how buyers' forecast-revision behavior affects delivery performance from customized-equipment suppliers, an analog for turbine slot booking.
Capacity and Inventory Management: Review, Trends, and Projections (Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 2019)
Reviews capacity and inventory research, a starting point for modeling OEM capacity expansion against long backlogs.
03 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Malaysia approves a 30% renewable energy requirement for data centers

DatacenterDynamics · Oct 29 · Read · ☆ Star

On 27 Oct, Deputy Prime Minister and energy transition minister Fadillah Yusof told Parliament that the government has approved a requirement for data centers to use at least 30% renewable energy, based on total capacity or energy consumption. The Energy Commission is drafting a framework covering self-generation and the 30% rule. Operators are encouraged to install their own solar so costs aren't passed on to other consumers. On 28 Oct he added that data centers need backup generators that can be used to manage demand when the grid is under strain.

BackgroundAs of August 2026, data centers in Malaysia had contracted 2,346 MW of electricity demand but used only 1,374 MW (58.5%). Approved supply capacity for data centers was 12.3 GW, and data center peak demand is projected to reach 8.39 GW by 2030. An earlier parliamentary reply said compliance could come through the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme (CRESS) and Solar for Self-Consumption. Maybank IB expects the rule to apply to newly approved data centers and to drive CRESS demand from 2028.
ProblemThe rule tries to decarbonize fast data center growth and protect other ratepayers from the costs. But the compliance basis (capacity vs consumption) is unclear, coverage of existing sites is unconfirmed, and no start date or penalties have been reported. Operators must choose between on-site solar, CRESS contracts and other options under that uncertainty, and the requirement may affect where they choose to build in Southeast Asia.
Where & whyThe problem sits at the grid-connection and procurement stage for new large loads. Approved and contracted capacity far exceeds actual use, so planners face a load-forecasting challenge. Regulators also want to avoid cross-subsidies. The friction is regulatory uncertainty plus limited on-site renewable potential relative to dense data center loads, which pushes operators toward market-based procurement schemes whose supply may be constrained.
Stakeholders
Data center operators and hyperscalers in Malaysia — Compliance cost and the choice of procurement route; site competitiveness versus Singapore and other hubs
Energy Commission (Suruhanjaya Tenaga) — Designing workable compliance rules and grid-stress provisions
Other electricity consumers — Avoiding tariff increases from data center grid costs
Renewable developers and CRESS suppliers — A new demand pool for corporate renewable supply from about 2028
Research hints
💡 Whether the 30% is measured on capacity or on consumption changes the economics a lot; a simple procurement model can show how.
💡 Cross-country comparison of renewable mandates for data centers, as a policy-design and siting lens.
💡 The 58.5% gap between contracted and used power is a load-forecasting and overbooking puzzle with parallels in US large-load tariffs.
Data leadsMalaysian Energy Commission data center supply/demand statistics and CRESS uptake, Malaysian parliamentary Hansard replies, and as a US comparison, state PUC large-load tariff dockets.
Keywordsdata center electricity demand · regulatory uncertainty · power purchase agreements · facility siting · load forecasting
Related research
Meeting Corporate Renewable Power Targets (Management Science, 2022)
Models how firms meet percentage renewable targets with a portfolio of VPPAs under uncertainty, directly relevant to operators planning how to hit a 30% mandate.
04 · Circular Economy

VW opens its first circular economy hub at the Zwickau EV plant, which faces possible closure after 2031

Reuters · Oct 28 · Read · ☆ Star

On 28 Oct, Volkswagen unveiled its first "Circular Economy Hub" at its all-electric Zwickau plant in Saxony. From 2027, 100 retrained workers will dismantle 4,100 vehicles a year. VW plans to invest up to €90M and Saxony has pledged €10.7M. The hub is meant to secure up to 1,000 jobs over the longer term, but about 8,000 jobs at the site are at risk and no vehicle production is planned beyond 2031.

BackgroundVW announced an overhaul in July that is expected to leave excess capacity at Zwickau from mid-2026. Group-wide, it plans 100,000 job cuts and possibly closing four German plants. VW brand executive Thomas Schaefer frames dismantling and recycling as "creating value," not running a junkyard. The Wuppertal Institute's Henning Wilts calls it a pilot whose commercial viability is unproven. In Saxony, the car industry is linked to one in four jobs and support for the far right is high.
ProblemVW is repurposing stranded vehicle-assembly capacity for dismantling and recycling. But 4,100 vehicles a year is small next to the plant's workforce, and company sources say recycling alone won't save the plant. The open question is whether circular operations can be a viable business or mainly serve as a politically motivated soft landing.
Where & whyThe issue arises at the end-of-life stage of the automotive closed loop. Supply of returned vehicles and components is uncertain in volume and condition, and dismantling is labor-intensive. Margins depend on recovered-material and parts value. Incentives are mixed: the firm wants to cut capacity, while the state subsidizes jobs in a politically sensitive region, so the siting and scale of the hub may reflect more than economics.
Stakeholders
Volkswagen — Reducing excess capacity and costs while building a circular materials and parts business
Zwickau workers (about 8,000 jobs at risk) — Job security after 2031; retraining into dismantling roles
Saxony state government — Regional employment and political stability; return on its €10.7M pledge
Parts and material recyclers / remanufacturing market — Competition from or supply via an OEM-run hub
Research hints
💡 Remanufacturing procurement under uncertain core condition: what does 4,100 vehicles a year imply for hub utilization?
💡 Repurposing brownfield capacity versus closing it: a real-options or capacity-transition lens.
💡 Compare OEM-run dismantling hubs with independent end-of-life vehicle recyclers on scale and profitability.
Data leadsEurostat end-of-life vehicle statistics, German KBA vehicle deregistration data, and VW annual and sustainability reports for capex and circularity metrics.
Keywordsclosed-loop supply chain · reverse logistics · secondary materials · capacity utilization · plant closure
Related research
Technical Note—Optimal Procurement in Remanufacturing Systems with Uncertain Used-Item Condition (Operations Research, 2023)
Studies how remanufacturers should acquire used items when condition is uncertain and the system is congested, the core intake problem for a dismantling hub.
New-Product Diffusion in Closed-Loop Supply Chains (Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 2020)
Models a producer selling new and remanufactured goods over a product life cycle, relevant to how an OEM balances recovered parts against new production.
05 · Logistics Decarbonization

Coty reports a 48% cut in air freight since 2019 but a 33% jump in air freight emissions in FY2026

Supply Chain Dive · Oct 28 · Read · ☆ Star

According to its FY2026 sustainability report, Coty has cut air freight by 48% since 2019 and aims to bring air freight reliance as close to zero as possible. However, air freight emissions rose 33% from FY2025 to FY2026 because of global supply chain disruptions. Coty says moving freight to sea and rail avoided an estimated 3% rise in air freight volumes. Road makes up 91% of shipped weight, and Coty put three EVs into service in Goiânia, Brazil.

BackgroundCoty is a beauty company with licensing deals with Burberry, Tiffany & Co. and Swarovski. It uses the EcoTransIT World emissions tool for route planning and truckload efficiency. All figures are self-reported. The article gives no absolute tonnage or emissions, does not say whether the 48% is measured by volume or by emissions, and sets no date for reaching near-zero air freight.
ProblemMoving from air to ocean or rail cuts emissions and cost but lengthens lead times and increases exposure to disruptions. When disruptions hit, firms fall back on air, which is what drove the 33% rise. This raises the question of how durable mode-shift commitments are, and how comparable self-reported logistics metrics are across firms.
Where & whyThe friction is at the transport-mode choice in outbound and inbound logistics. Slower modes need more pipeline inventory and better forecasting. When disruptions occur, expediting by air becomes the costly safety valve. Disclosure gaps (no units or baselines) create an information asymmetry for investors and raters assessing scope 3 progress.
Stakeholders
Coty — Cutting logistics emissions and cost without losing service levels for high-value, seasonal products
Licensing partners (e.g., Burberry, Tiffany & Co.) — Product availability and brand-level sustainability claims
Ocean, rail and air carriers — Shifts in volume between modes
Investors and ESG raters — Verifiable, comparable scope 3 transport data
Research hints
💡 Treat air freight as an expediting option: inventory models with dual modes (fast vs slow supply) fit naturally.
💡 Notice the puzzle of a falling long-run trend alongside a one-year spike: disruptions can quickly reverse progress on logistics decarbonization.
💡 Compare how transport metrics are disclosed across consumer goods firms, through a greenwashing or disclosure-quality lens.
Data leadsCoty's FY2026 sustainability report and SEC 10-K, CDP climate responses for scope 3 category 4/9 transport emissions, and bill-of-lading data (Panjiva) or Census trade data by transport mode.
Keywordsscope 3 disclosure · voluntary disclosure · mode shift · freight rates · inventory management
Related research
Shipping to Heterogeneous Customers with Competing Carriers (Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 2019)
Models a shipper choosing between fast and slow competing carriers for time-sensitive goods, the core speed–cost trade-off in Coty's air-to-ocean shift.
Introducing synchromodality: One missing link between transportation and supply chain management (Journal of Supply Chain Management, 2021)
Develops the synchromodality concept of flexibly switching modes on real-time information, relevant to when Coty reverts to air during disruptions.
Thread to pull

Several of today's items are capacity problems at a single interface: railcars at the port–rail handoff, turbine factory slots for data centers, and renewable supply for Malaysia's 30% mandate. In each, scarcity may push buyers to book early or overbook, as with Malaysia's 58.5% use of contracted power and the full 2028–2029 turbine slots. Comparing how slot reservation and contracted-versus-used capacity play out across ports, equipment and grids could be a productive line to explore.

New in the journals

From Employer Responsibility to Environmental Irresponsibility: Unintended Impacts of Social Insurance Law on Pollution Emissions
Management Science

Using difference-in-differences around China's 2011 Social Insurance Law, the paper finds that higher mandated social insurance contributions increased firms' industrial pollution (chemical oxygen demand and wastewater). The mechanism is that rising labor costs and financial distress led firms to cut spending on end-of-pipe treatment and on cleaner production measures.

It is a template for studying spillovers between policy domains, for example whether VW's labor-driven restructuring or Malaysia's renewables mandate shifts effort away from other operational or environmental goals.
