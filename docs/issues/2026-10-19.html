01 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Startup forecasts 57% jump in 2026 emissions for five hyperscalers, using capex to predict emissions before disclosure

Trellis · Oct 19 · Read · ☆ Star

Bricola Group, a startup founded by former Amazon economist Jimmy Gillan, forecasts that combined emissions of Alphabet, AWS, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle will rise 57% in 2026 to 215 million tCO2e gross. Net of renewable energy certificates and similar purchases, the forecast is 140 million tCO2e, up from 87 million in 2025; AWS and Alphabet are each expected at just over 53 million tCO2e. The model maps past capex to past emissions. Capex is reported in 10-Ks 5–9 months before sustainability reports, and the five firms' combined 2025–2026 capex is expected to exceed $1 trillion. These are first-use model estimates with no outside validation, and Alphabet, AWS and Meta rely on capex guidance rather than reported figures.

OM angleCapex-to-emissions mapping is a forward-looking emissions estimator, and it can be tested against what firms later report. The forecast also measures how far AI capacity growth outruns corporate decarbonization, including the gap between gross and certificate-adjusted (market-based) accounting.
Research QHow well does capital expenditure predict a firm's later-reported Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions, and does the predictive gap widen during capacity build-outs such as AI data centers?
Data / IDBuild a firm-year panel of capex and PP&E from Compustat or SEC EDGAR 10-Ks, matched to emissions in CDP responses and sustainability reports. Estimate out-of-sample prediction errors before and after 2023, treating the AI capex surge as the shock and comparing hyperscalers with other capex-heavy firms. Compare location-based emissions with market-based (certificate-adjusted) emissions, and add facility-level power-sector context from EPA eGRID.

02 · Circular Economy

Experts argue better sorting and updated bottle bills could ease US aluminum shortage more cheaply than new smelters

Canary Media · Oct 19 · Read · ☆ Star

Amid a global aluminum shortage and US tariffs, Canary Media reports that millions of tons of US aluminum scrap still end up in landfills. Recycled aluminum needs only 5% of the energy of primary metal, while new or restarted smelters must compete with data centers for cheap power. SAFE's report says the US imports 84% of its primary aluminum, runs only five smelters in 2026, and faces primary demand rising from 4.4 to 5.1 million tons by 2029. Proposed fixes include Sortera's alloy-sorting plants (a $45M raise for a second site in Tennessee) and updating the 10 state bottle bills; Massachusetts' 5-cent deposit is 43 years old.

OM angleThis is a concrete primary-versus-secondary sourcing trade-off in which energy prices, tariffs and collection policy all set the economics of closed-loop supply. Sorting quality decides whether scrap becomes high-value alloy or is downcycled into low-value products.
Research QDo states with bottle-deposit laws, and higher deposit values, achieve higher aluminum recovery and lower landfill disposal, and does that translate into more domestic secondary aluminum supply?
Data / IDRun state-level panels comparing the 10 deposit states with non-deposit states, using EPA materials and waste data, state recycling and EPR program reports, and changes in deposit law over time. Track aluminum scrap exports and imports by HS code in Census USA Trade Online to see whether higher tariffs shift scrap toward domestic remelting.

03 · Critical Minerals & Batteries

US reportedly weighing critical-minerals price floor with allies; reports conflict on whether it will proceed

Stocktwits · Oct 18 · Read · ☆ Star

Citing Bloomberg, Stocktwits reported a multi-agency US plan for a critical-minerals price floor, to be rolled out through the Pax Silica program after a 55-country summit, with reported agreements with the EU, Japan and Mexico. MP, USAR, UAMY and TMC each rose more than 2% pre-market. On January 28, Reuters had reported the administration was stepping back from price floors because Congress had not funded them, and those stocks fell 5–10% after hours. MP Materials said its own $110/kg floor contract for light rare earths remains in force. The article is stamped March 5, 2026, so it is roughly seven months old, and the policy's current status is unverified.

OM angleA guaranteed minimum price is a market-design intervention that shifts demand risk from producers to the government, which should change capacity investment and buyers' sourcing decisions. The back-and-forth news flow gives clean event dates for testing that.
Research QHow do announcements and reversals of price-floor support change rare-earth producers' valuations and their announced capacity investments, compared with downstream buyers'?
Data / IDRun an event study on the January 28 Reuters report and the Bloomberg price-floor report, using CRSP returns for producers (MP, USAR, UAMY, TMC, CRML) against downstream users identified through Compustat customer segments or FactSet Revere. Follow up with capex and capacity announcements in SEC 8-Ks and 10-Ks, and benchmark against USGS mineral data.

04 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Microsoft signs 20-year, 1.1 GW deal with NiSource for Indiana data center, paying for all new infrastructure

DatacenterDynamics · Oct 19 · Read · ☆ Star

Microsoft agreed to a 20-year contract with NiSource for about 1,100 MW of generation for a northwest Indiana data center campus, starting in 2028. DCD thinks the site is likely La Porte, but this is unconfirmed. Power flows through NiSource's GenCo subsidiary under a new rate structure for very large customers. Microsoft pays for all required generation and transmission, and GenCo has contracted 600 MW of battery storage. NiSource raised its estimate of savings for existing customers from its data center contracts to about $1.75B, up from $1.4B, or up to $140 per customer per year.

OM angleUnder this 'pay your own way' structure, a dedicated generation subsidiary and the large customer carry the build-out risk. That is a contract design for allocating capacity risk between hyperscalers and regulated ratepayers, and it is likely to spread to other utilities.
Research QDo dedicated large-load tariffs and generation-subsidiary structures like NiSource's GenCo shield residential rates from data center load growth, compared with utilities that serve data centers under standard tariffs?
Data / IDCompare utility-level residential rates and sales in EIA-861 for utilities adopting large-load tariffs versus those that have not, with adoption dates taken from state PUC dockets such as the Indiana IURC NIPSCO filings. Add generator and storage additions from EIA-860 to measure the capacity actually built per contracted MW.
