FERC rejects PJM's curtailment-based large-load interconnection plan, approves data center registry

Utility Dive · Oct 12 · Read

On Oct. 9, 2026, FERC rejected the core of PJM's plan for connecting data centers and other large loads (accession no. 20261009-3055). The rejected Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS) would have let large loads connect without matching new capacity if they accepted curtailment ahead of grid emergencies. FERC found it intruded on state authority over retail rates and appeared to discriminate against large loads, but it approved PJM's large-load definition and a registry of each facility's location, ramp schedule and capacity supply. Jefferies expects PJM capacity prices to stay higher for longer and any revival to be state-led, with Ohio and Pennsylvania the ones to watch; the ruling follows FERC's rejection about five weeks earlier of key parts of PJM's backstop procurement auction.

OM angleThe order shifts curtailment-priority decisions to the states and creates a facility-level registry of large loads, a potential new dataset. Commissioner Rosner warned that leaving curtailable loads out of the capacity auction demand curve could make the auction signal ample capacity even as the region involuntarily sheds thousands of megawatts.

02 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Utilities use collateral, deposits and longer contracts to filter speculative 'phantom' data center power requests

WSJ · Oct 13 · Read

According to a Breitbart write-up of a paywalled WSJ report (figures not verified against the WSJ text), Wood Mackenzie rates only 23% of disclosed data center power requests as high confidence. Large-customer requests to Exelon tripled to 18,000 MW. After Exelon required binding commitments and collateral, its high-probability list fell to 11,000 MW, backed by about $1 billion in posted collateral. In Texas, the connection queue exceeds 400,000 MW, large customers now pay a $50,000/MW study deposit, and minimum large-customer contract terms nationally average about 10.8 years versus about 4.6 before 2024 (per Halcyon); Vistra's CEO says demand estimates are overstated by more than 20 times.

OM angleThis is a clean setting for screening and demand-signaling under capacity investment uncertainty: collateral and contract length act as costly signals that separate serious projects from speculative ones. The figures are second-hand, so treat them as hypotheses to verify.

03 · Supply Chain Resilience

Taylor Farms halts May–August central Mexico lettuce sourcing after a Cyclospora outbreak linked to nearly 13,000 cases

Supply Chain Dive · Oct 13 · Read

In an Oct. 8 letter, Taylor Farms CEO Bruce Taylor said the company will indefinitely stop harvesting or processing central Mexico iceberg lettuce for the U.S. and Canada during the CDC's Cyclospora season (May 1–Aug. 31), which coincides with the region's rainy season. Cases linked to Taco Bell or to recalled Taylor Farms lettuce reached nearly 13,000 this summer. The FDA closed its investigation on Oct. 2 without establishing the contamination route, though it said traceback evidence pointed to shredded iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms de Mexico. The company is also expanding water and crop testing, funding detection research and joining an FDA-led task force.

OM angleThis is a seasonal, risk-based sourcing exclusion rather than a full exit, a firm-level response to a food safety disruption whose root cause was never established. It raises questions about whether such responses come from learning or from signaling.

04 · Trade & Industrial Policy

FreightWaves: revocation of about 25,000 Mexican drivers' B-1 visas tightens US–Mexico border trucking capacity

FreightWaves · Oct 13 · Read

FreightWaves reports that U.S. authorities have revoked B-1 visas for about 25,000 Mexican truck drivers; the revoking agency and the timing were not stated, and the figure is unverified. SONAR data show El Paso tender rejections peaking at about 24–25% in early July and still near 20%, San Diego/Otay Mesa at about 10% versus 3.9% a year ago, and Laredo rising from 6.07% (Oct. 3) to 7.78% (Oct. 10). Year-over-year spot rates are up 57% on San Diego–Phoenix and 43% on Laredo–Dallas. Mexico freight volumes are fairly stable, so FreightWaves attributes the pressure to lost driver supply and expects tight conditions to last well into 2027.

OM angleA labor-supply shock to a specific mode and geography, while demand stays roughly flat, offers a rare chance to separate capacity effects from demand effects in freight markets. Canada lanes, where the issue is a freight imbalance rather than a driver shortage, offer a contrast.

Compare Mexico border lanes with Canada border and interior lanes in an event study around the revocation timing, once the date is pinned down from DHS/State records. Shipper-level responses could be traced with bill-of-lading data.

05 · Circular Economy

Lollicup's challenge to Oregon's packaging EPR law to be paused pending NAW's Ninth Circuit appeal

Packaging Dive · Oct 13 · Read

On Oct. 9, foodservice ware supplier Lollicup USA and Oregon DEQ director Leah Feldon jointly moved to stay Lollicup's June class action over Oregon's packaging EPR law. The stay would last until the Ninth Circuit decides the National Association of Wholesaler-Distributors' appeal, after Judge Michael Simon upheld the law in August. NAW's opening brief is due Dec. 17, and status reports would begin April 16, 2027. An Arnold & Porter partner noted that companies must still comply in the meantime, and that a Lollicup–DEQ enforcement-limiting agreement appears to have expired Aug. 31, 2026; EPR suits are also pending in California and Colorado.

OM angleLegal uncertainty over state packaging EPR programs is a live friction for producers' compliance, fee and packaging-design decisions. The appellate timeline creates identifiable windows in which that uncertainty resolves.
