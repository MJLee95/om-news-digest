01 · ESG & Disclosure

UK FCA finalizes rules bringing ESG ratings providers under supervision from June 2028

ESG Today · Nov 03 · Read · ☆ Star

The Financial Conduct Authority published final rules (PS26/22) on transparency, governance and conflicts of interest for ESG ratings providers, putting them under UK supervision for the first time. Applications for authorization open in June 2027, and from June 2028 any firm providing ESG ratings in the UK, whether UK-based or foreign, will need FCA authorization.

BackgroundThe UK government finalized the law making the FCA responsible for ESG ratings providers in October 2025, and the FCA proposed draft rules in late 2025. The final regime draws on IOSCO recommendations and the ICMA Code of Conduct for ESG ratings and data product providers. Providers must publicly disclose methodologies, objectives, coverage, the set of entities rated, data approach, limitations and how they engage with rated companies. Users must get advance notice of material methodology changes, and rated entities must get advance notice of new ratings and more detail on request.
ProblemESG ratings feed investment and corporate decisions, yet methodologies have been opaque and providers can have conflicts of interest. The regime tries to improve trust through disclosure, governance and conflict management. Whether this lowers rating disagreement or improves rating quality, and what compliance requires (including an 'appropriate UK presence'), is open. The cost to firms and the number of providers affected are not yet confirmed.
Where & whyThe friction sits in the information intermediary layer between rated companies and investors. It stems from information asymmetry (opaque methods), misaligned incentives (conflicts when providers sell other services) and weak accountability for outsourced processes. The regime's requirement that providers keep full responsibility even when parts are outsourced targets that last point.
Stakeholders
ESG ratings providers (UK and foreign) — Face authorization, disclosure and governance costs; some may exit or restructure UK offerings.
Rated companies — Gain advance notice of new ratings and more detail on request, which could change how they engage with raters.
Investors and rating users — Want more comparable and trustworthy ratings, plus advance notice of methodology changes.
FCA — Must supervise a new sector credibly, aligned with IOSCO and ICMA standards.
Research hints
💡 Staggered timeline (law Oct 2025, applications June 2027, effect June 2028) offers several event windows; compare raters that serve the UK market with those that don't.
💡 Credit rating agency regulation after 2008 (EU CRA rules, Dodd-Frank) is a natural analogue for predicting effects on concentration and rating informativeness.
💡 Mandatory methodology disclosure may reduce cross-rater disagreement, or may simply make the sources of disagreement more visible.
Data leadsMSCI ESG and LSEG ESG scores (via university) to track rating changes and cross-rater disagreement before and after the rules; FCA register of authorized firms once applications open; RepRisk incidents as an external benchmark.
Keywordsesg rating disagreement · rating agency regulation · conflicts of interest · voluntary disclosure · sustainable finance taxonomy
02 · Energy & AI Data Centers

PJM refiles data center backstop procurement as utilities cut large-load forecasts by 6.5 GW

Utility Dive · Nov 03 · Read · ☆ Star

In late October 2026, PJM filed a revised reliability backstop procurement (RBP) with FERC to fill a 6.8 GW capacity shortfall, with the process set to start March 1, about seven months later than first planned. Jefferies analysts estimate the procurement could shrink to roughly 630 MW once PJM recalculates its needs. The cut follows a 6.5 GW reduction in near-term large-load forecasts by utilities including PPL Electric, FirstEnergy and AES Ohio.

BackgroundPJM's first proposal, filed July 31, covered the 6,831 MW shortfall from the 2028/29 base residual auction, with contracts of up to 15 years and an average cost cap of $555/MW-day. FERC rejected most of it in late September and told PJM to refile or face a five-month paper hearing. PJM says the revisions are 'carefully tailored' to FERC's concerns. About a month ago, the DOE urged PJM to allocate costs based on updated load forecasts so they don't shift onto existing customers. PJM has hired the consultancy CRA for an independent data center demand forecast.
ProblemHow much long-term capacity to procure, and who pays for it, depends on highly uncertain data center load forecasts. If forecasts are overstated, existing ratepayers could be locked into up to 15-year contracts for capacity that isn't needed. If they are understated, reliability suffers. Bilateral deals between developers and power suppliers could shrink the need further. FERC has not ruled on the revised filing, and the 630 MW figure is an analyst estimate, not a PJM number.
Where & whyThe problem arises where utility load forecasts feed RTO capacity procurement. Large-load requests can be speculative or duplicated across utilities, creating information asymmetry between data center developers and planners. Utilities and developers have little incentive to discount uncertain demand when costs are spread across all customers. Regulatory delay adds further uncertainty for both investment and procurement timing.
Stakeholders
PJM Interconnection — Needs a procurement design FERC will accept that secures reliability without overbuying.
Utilities (PPL Electric, FirstEnergy, AES Ohio) — Forecast revisions shift their exposure to procurement costs and risk.
Existing residential and commercial customers — Risk paying for capacity procured for data center load that may not arrive.
Data center developers — Want firm power; may bypass the backstop through bilateral supply contracts.
FERC and DOE — Oversee fair cost allocation and grid reliability.
Research hints
💡 Treat the backstop as capacity investment under demand uncertainty with a forecast-quality problem; think about who bears the cost of forecast error under each cost-allocation rule.
💡 Compare utility-submitted forecasts with the independent CRA forecast to measure systematic bias in large-load projections.
💡 The drop from 6.8 GW to a possible 0.6 GW is a striking case of forecast revisions; ask what drives large-load forecast churn (speculative, duplicate or withdrawn requests).
Data leadsFERC eLibrary for the RBP filings and orders; PJM capacity auction results and load forecast reports; state PUC dockets on large-load tariffs; EIA-861 for utility-level sales.
Keywordsdata center electricity demand · load forecasting · capacity markets · large-load tariffs · cost pass-through
Related research
Supplier Competition with Option Contracts for Discrete Blocks of Capacity (Operations Research, 2017)
Models competition among suppliers offering option contracts on discrete capacity blocks under uncertain demand, which maps onto backstop procurement of MW blocks.
Application-Driven Learning: A Closed-Loop Prediction and Optimization Approach Applied to Dynamic Reserves and Demand Forecasting (Operations Research, 2024)
Tailors forecast models to downstream reserve-procurement costs, relevant to how load forecasts should be judged when they drive capacity decisions.
03 · Critical Minerals & Batteries

India approves Rs 2,814 crore, 30% capex subsidy auction for four lithium and nickel processing plants

PM India · Nov 04 · Read · ☆ Star

On November 4, 2026, India's Union Cabinet approved the Capex Support Scheme to Promote Critical Mineral Processing, worth Rs 2,814 crore over FY2026-27 to FY2030-31. It covers 30% of eligible investment for two lithium plants of at least 30 kt each and two nickel plants of at least 50 kt each. Support is capped at Rs 500 crore per lithium beneficiary and Rs 900 crore per nickel beneficiary.

BackgroundIndia depends on imported refined critical minerals; the release names lithium, nickel and cobalt. The scheme targets the midstream step of turning ores or intermediate products into high-purity compounds. Selection has two stages: applicants first pass a technical qualification, then bid, and the two winners per mineral are those requesting the lowest capex support. Payments come in three tranches tied to project milestones.
ProblemThe government wants domestic refining capacity built at the lowest subsidy cost, but midstream processing has scale economies, technology risk and exposure to volatile global prices. Lowest-bid selection may attract aggressive bids that later fail to deliver, and milestone payments only partly guard against this. No companies, bidding timeline or plant locations have been named.
Where & whyThe gap sits at the refining stage between upstream ore and downstream battery manufacturing, where global capacity is concentrated outside India. Private investors under-invest because of high capital costs and price uncertainty, and the government faces information asymmetry about each bidder's true costs. That asymmetry is what motivates the reverse-auction design.
Stakeholders
Government of India — Wants supply security for refined minerals at minimal fiscal cost.
Prospective bidders (miners, refiners, conglomerates) — Compete for up to 30% capex support but carry technology and price risk.
Downstream battery and EV manufacturers — Could gain domestic high-purity inputs and less import exposure.
Incumbent foreign refiners and suppliers — Could lose Indian demand if domestic capacity comes online.
Research hints
💡 Procurement auction theory, including scoring rules, winner's curse and non-delivery risk, applies directly to 'lowest subsidy requested' selection with milestone tranches.
💡 Compare with US production tax credits and DOE grants for midstream minerals: capex share versus output-based subsidies.
💡 Track later trade flows of refined lithium and nickel compounds into India to see whether import dependence actually falls.
Data leadsUN Comtrade for Indian imports of refined lithium and nickel compounds; USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries for global refining capacity; Benchmark/BNEF (commercial) for plant-level pipelines.
Keywordsindustrial policy · raw material security · procurement auctions · nickel supply chain · capacity investment under uncertainty
Related research
Benefits of Collaboration on Capacity Investment and Allocation (Production and Operations Management, 2024)
Studies capacity investment made before uncertainty resolves and collaboration between competitors, relevant to a few subsidized refiners committing to large plants.
04 · Supply Chain Resilience

3M has closed 16 sites in 2026 and will act on 10 more to reach $1B in productivity gains

Supply Chain Dive · Nov 03 · Read · ☆ Star

On its October 20, 2026 Q3 earnings call, CEO Bill Brown said 3M had closed 16 manufacturing sites and distribution centers so far this year and will act on 10 more over the next 12 months. The moves support a target of $1 billion in annual productivity gains by end-2027. 3M also aims to cut days of inventory from the high 90s to the mid-70s, with each day worth about $35 million.

BackgroundThe productivity program launched about a year ago. Early savings came partly from procurement and logistics, and network consolidation is expected to deliver the rest by end-FY2027. 3M plans more than $250 million over three years on automation across factories and distribution centers. Q3 gross margin rose 230 basis points year over year, and full-year adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $9.10–$9.15. Reports differ on whether all 10 additional sites will close (Supply Chain Dive says 'changes'; one summary says 'closures'), and the specific sites and any job cuts are unconfirmed.
ProblemConsolidating plants and DCs, cutting inventory by about 20+ days and automating raise efficiency. But they also reduce redundancy and buffer stock, which can increase exposure to disruptions and longer distances to customers. The trade-off between cost savings and supply chain resilience is the core tension.
Where & whyThe decision sits at the network design and inventory policy level of a diversified manufacturer. It is driven by pressure on margins and returns after years of complexity. Fewer nodes concentrate production (common-mode risk), and lower safety stock leaves less buffer against demand or supply shocks.
Stakeholders
3M management and shareholders — Seek $1B in productivity gains, a better margin and cash freed from inventory.
Customers — May face changes in lead times or service levels as the network shrinks.
Workers and local communities at affected sites — Potential job losses; not yet confirmed.
Automation and logistics suppliers — Gain from more than $250M in automation spending.
Research hints
💡 Facility location and network consolidation models with disruption risk offer a lens on the efficiency-resilience frontier.
💡 Compare inventory days and service outcomes of firms that consolidated networks with peers that did not, using public financials.
💡 Puzzle: does cutting inventory at the same time as closing nodes compound risk, or does a simpler network need less buffer?
Data leadsCompustat (days inventory, margins) and earnings call transcripts; SEC EDGAR 10-K property and restructuring disclosures; state WARN notices for site-level closures.
Keywordsplant closure · inventory management · capacity utilization · network consolidation · common-mode failure
Related research

Examines drivers of days-of-supply differences across automakers, a template for analyzing 3M's inventory-days target.
Order from chaos: A meta‐analysis of supply chain complexity and firm performance (Journal of Supply Chain Management, 2021)
A meta-analysis linking supply chain complexity to firm performance, relevant to the logic of shrinking network complexity.
05 · Food & Agriculture

EPA abandons 20-year effort to set air-emission estimating methods for large livestock operations

Inside Climate News · Nov 03 · Read · ☆ Star

Around October 30, 2026, EPA said it will not finalize emissions-estimating methods developed under the 2005 Animal Feeding Operations Air Compliance Agreement and will move to end the agreement. EPA plans to notify about 14,000 hog, poultry and dairy farms, linked to roughly 2,600 animal feeding operations, before ending it. EPA said the roughly 20-year-old monitoring data are too limited and outdated to produce credible estimates.

BackgroundThe agreement gave participating farms an interim reprieve while the industry-funded National Air Emissions Monitoring Study produced data for estimation methods. The National Milk Producers Federation argued the draft dairy models rested on monitoring data from only five dairies. Separately, the farms' reporting requirements under CERCLA and EPCRA had already been gutted by the Trump administration and Congress. Existing Clean Air Act obligations remain, so facilities above emissions thresholds legally still need permits.
ProblemWithout accepted estimation methods, neither farms nor regulators can reliably tell which operations exceed permitting thresholds. Permit obligations therefore exist on paper but are hard to enforce. Industry welcomes regulatory certainty, while critics see an end to any attempt to regulate factory farm air pollution under the Clean Air Act. Whether a Federal Register notice or legal challenges will follow is unconfirmed.
Where & whyThe failure is in measurement: emissions from livestock operations are diffuse and vary by practice, and the monitoring sample was small and dated. That creates an information gap that blocks threshold-based enforcement. Industry funding of the study and long regulatory delay point to incentive and coordination problems in how the measurement effort was run.
Stakeholders
Livestock and dairy producers (NMPF, pork groups) — Gain 'a clear, final answer' and avoid compliance costs from flawed models.
EPA — Ends a stalled program but retains nominal Clean Air Act authority.
Nearby communities and environmental advocates — Lose a path to emissions oversight and information on local air pollution.
Food companies sourcing livestock products — Scope 3 emissions accounting for animal agriculture stays reliant on rough estimates.
Research hints
💡 Regulation built on thresholds depends on measurement; consider how estimation uncertainty affects compliance when firms self-assess.
💡 Compare with dairy digester and methane programs at the state level (e.g. California) as a contrast in oversight intensity.
💡 Remote sensing of farm emissions could substitute for the abandoned facility monitoring; think about what this means for supply chain emissions disclosure.
Data leadsFederal Register for any formal notice; USDA NASS Quick Stats for livestock inventories by county; EPA GHGRP and state air permit databases for CAFO permits; satellite methane products as a complement.
Keywordsregulatory enforcement · regulatory thresholds · emissions monitoring · supplier emissions · scope 3 disclosure
Thread to pull

PJM's backstop and India's processing scheme both commit public or ratepayer money to long-lived capacity before demand is known. PJM faces forecast inflation from data center requests, while India uses a lowest-subsidy reverse auction to screen bidders. EPA's livestock retreat shows the flip side: when measurement is weak, regulation built on thresholds stalls. Together they raise a common question of how procurement and regulatory design should account for the quality of the underlying information.

New in the journals

Rethinking Supplier Integration in Humanitarian Supply Chains: Feasibility and Constraints From an International Humanitarian Organization Perspective
Journal of Operations Management

Using an embedded single-case study of a leading international humanitarian organization, the paper asks whether supplier integration practices from commercial supply chains work in humanitarian supply chains. These operate under extreme uncertainty, demand volatility, infrastructure disruption and resource scarcity across many actors, and the study assesses the feasibility of supplier integration and its constraints in that setting.

It offers a contrast to 3M's efficiency-driven consolidation: in highly uncertain settings, supplier relationships and integration are themselves a source of resilience.
