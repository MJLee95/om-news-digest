Week ending November 8

The week was dominated by how the US and UK grid systems absorb uncertain AI data center load. Stories covered anticipatory substations, PJM's shrinking backstop procurement, ERCOT's 75 MW audit cutoff, a Senate 'large loads pay' rule at 20 MW, and 37 of 38 called local ballot measures going against development. A second thread was policy reversal and loosening, including Brazil's disclosure U-turn, the dropped EU Green Claims Directive, the weaker EU car CO2 target and EPA abandoning livestock emissions methods. Several firm-level responses to concentrated supply shocks also stood out: Apple's $33B in added memory commitments, Indian Oil buying its own tankers, a 45% ammonia price spike and repeated cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration.

Research ideas

Idea 1

Gaming the megawatt line: bunching of data center load requests at regulatory thresholds

QuestionDid ERCOT's August 3, 2026 audit, which pauses loads of 75 MW or more and only surveys 25–75 MW loads, cause developers to cluster just below 75 MW, split projects, or shift to behind-the-meter generation? Do similar responses appear at the 25 MW local caps and the proposed 20 MW federal cost rule?
Why nowThe ERCOT audit created a sharp, dated cutoff, and there are reports (unverified) that ERCOT is probing downsizing. The Nov 3 ballot measures added municipal caps at 25 MW, and the Senate bill would add a 20 MW cost rule. Multiple thresholds with different dates let a researcher separate threshold effects from standard module sizes.
DataERCOT large-load interconnection status reports (project-level MW and dates); PUCT audit dockets; LBNL interconnection queue data for PJM, MISO and SPP as control distributions; EIA-860 for new onsite gas units near ERCOT data center clusters; Ballotpedia local measure results for the 25 MW caps.
IdentificationCompare the bunching estimate of excess mass just below 75 MW before versus after Aug 3, 2026. Difference against the same MW bins in other ISOs' queues, which faced no 75 MW rule, to net out common module sizes. As a second margin, test for a rise in co-located onsite generation filings among projects just under the cutoff. The main threat is that 50–75 MW is already a standard hyperscale building block, and that queue entries are revised or withdrawn for reasons unrelated to the audit. The pre-period and cross-ISO counterfactual distributions are essential, as is handling phased projects that the same developer files several times.
LiteratureThe Effect of Discretion on Procurement Performance offers a template for how regulatory discontinuities both change outcomes and invite manipulation of the running variable; this setting adds an information-quality cost, since manipulation degrades the grid operator's forecast.

Idea 2

Forward commitments versus inventory versus pass-through when AI demand crowds out component supply

QuestionWhen AI server demand drives a memory price shock, how do downstream hardware firms split their response among higher purchase obligations, inventory buffers and price increases? Do larger buyers' advance commitments crowd smaller firms out of supply, showing up as worse margins or stockouts?
Why nowApple disclosed a $33B quarter-on-quarter rise in purchase obligations, about 1 point of margin lost to memory, and price increases. This happened as upstream equipment makers such as Caterpillar report AI-driven demand surges, and as Nvidia rations scarce supply by customer mix. The timing of the memory shock is sharp and common across firms, while exposure (memory share of the bill of materials) varies across firms.
DataPurchase-obligation footnotes in 10-K/10-Q filings on SEC EDGAR for PC, phone, server and networking firms; Compustat inventory days, gross margins and sales; earnings call transcripts coded for memory, price increases and supply mentions; Census USA Trade Online imports of memory ICs as an aggregate price and quantity check.
IdentificationUse a firm-level difference-in-differences, comparing changes in purchase obligations, inventory and gross margin across firms with high versus low memory intensity before versus after the onset of the price surge. Add firm size or buyer share at shared suppliers as a moderator to test for crowd-out. The main threat is that AI demand also boosts sales at server-exposed firms, confounding the supply-side story. Separate server makers from consumer-device makers and control for segment demand growth. A secondary issue is that purchase-obligation disclosures vary in scope across firms.
LiteratureTo Share or Not to Share? Capacity Reservation in a Shared Supplier models how buyers competing for a common supplier reserve capacity; Competitive Options, Supply Contracting, and Electronic Markets frames the spot-versus-contract choice that Apple's disclosure makes observable.

Idea 3

Does regulatory whiplash deter voluntary sustainability reporting? Evidence from Brazil

QuestionAfter Brazil replaced its ISSB-aligned mandate with comply-or-explain in May 2026, which listed firms kept reporting voluntarily? Did the three-year lock-in and the notice required to stop reporting deter opt-in among firms that had already invested in reporting systems?
Why nowThe CVM's sequence of mandate, then comply-or-explain, then a consultation (open to Feb 28, 2027) on re-mandating gives repeated, dated policy shocks within one market. The regulator explicitly lists stranded investment and deterrence by opt-in conditions as hypotheses it will test. Elsewhere, the EU dropping Green Claims and net-zero backtracking show the same durability problem.
DataCVM filings (Formulário de Referência) and comply-or-explain market announcements; consultation comment letters; CDP responses and LSEG/MSCI ESG coverage for Brazilian issuers as measures of prior reporting investment; B3 price data for event studies.
IdentificationBuild a cross-section of opt-in decisions under the voluntary regime. The key regressor is pre-period readiness (prior CDP or GRI reporting, assurance relationships), interacted with firm size and foreign investor ownership. Event studies at the May 2026 reversal and the consultation opening would capture valuation effects of the mandate's credibility. If a size-based phase-in is adopted, a later regression-discontinuity design becomes possible. The main threat is self-selection: firms that opt in may simply have better ESG performance or more international investors, so lock-in deterrence is hard to separate from low expected benefit. Comment letters can help, because they reveal firms' stated reasons.
LiteratureMarket Reaction to Mandatory Nonfinancial Disclosure and Shareholder Wealth Effects of Modern Slavery Regulation provide event-study templates for disclosure mandates; Brazil's reversal lets one test whether those valuation effects reverse symmetrically when a mandate is withdrawn.

Patterns this week

Hard regulatory size thresholds for large loads (and firms)

Four separate regimes this week sort data centers by a megawatt cutoff: local bans at 25 MW, ERCOT's audit at 75 MW, the Senate bill's cost-responsibility rule at 20 MW, and the UK's £350m revenue line for due diligence. Sharp cutoffs reward splitting, downsizing or moving power behind the meter, and that degrades the information planners receive. For OM researchers these thresholds are ready-made running variables for bunching and discontinuity designs, and they also pose a mechanism-design question: how should screening rules be set when the party being screened chooses its own reported size?

Who bears AI demand uncertainty along the power and component chain

The same uncertain AI load shows up at every stage of the chain. National Grid is building 3.2 GW for a tenant not yet named. PJM's 6.8 GW shortfall may shrink to about 0.6 GW after utilities cut their forecasts by 6.5 GW. Caterpillar is expanding engine and turbine output, and Apple is locking in memory supply through purchase obligations. In each case the open question is how forecast risk is allocated (socialized rates, collateral, option-like commitments, onsite generation), which is a classic capacity-investment and contracting problem with unusually observable data this cycle.

Sustainability rules reversing or softening, with some tightening at the margins

Several policies moved backward this week. Brazil's CVM is reconsidering a mandate it shelved months ago, the EU dropped verified green claims, and the EU Parliament's environment committee cut the 2035 car target to 87.5%. EPA abandoned its livestock emissions methods, and 20 of the 50 largest firms show signs of backtracking on net-zero targets. Fleets are delaying truck orders amid uncertainty over EPA's 2027 NOx rules, and DOE launched a battery strategy just after the EV credit lapsed. The UK moved the other way, regulating ESG raters and mandating forced-labour due diligence. Repeated reversals create dated policy shocks, and they make policy durability itself a variable that can be measured in firms' capex, disclosure and order timing.

Concentrated and common-mode supply exposure

Several shocks this week spread through a single shared node: one controller platform across grocery chains in several countries, Trinidad gas for US ammonia, memory capacity shared between AI servers and phones, and the Hormuz chokepoint for Indian crude freight. Two others show the same weakness. Iranian and Turkish pistachio crops failed together, and US rules on grid equipment now exclude suppliers by country rather than by technical standard. Firm responses varied widely, from vertical integration (IOC buying tankers) to forward commitments (Apple) to absorbing the cost (farmers), which gives a cross-section on how firms respond to concentration.
