Daily Clippings · Weekly review

Week ending November 1

AI data-center power dominated the week from two directions. Jurisdictions issued sharply different rules for large new loads (Spain, Malaysia, CAISO, Texas, Virginia transmission), while the supply side showed multi-year lock-in: Anthropic's S-1 commitments, gas turbine deliveries pushed to about 2031, and BNEF's 30% storage revision. Alongside it, fights over how granular sustainability claims must be (Scope 2 hourly matching, carrier-level shipping emissions, cement certificates) and the EUDR's Dec 30 enforcement date set up clean policy shocks. Transport chokepoints (the Mexican trucking strike, LA–Long Beach rail backlog, Hormuz) and circular-economy collection bottlenecks recurred across days.

Research ideas

Idea 1

Does conditional interconnection change where and how data centers build?

QuestionWhen a grid operator offers large loads early but curtailable interconnection (as CAISO does), rather than freezing hookups (Texas) or offering only firm service, how do data-center developers change (a) where they file large-load queue requests, (b) how fast they withdraw speculative requests, and (c) whether they co-locate batteries or gas behind the meter?
Why nowA single FERC show-cause order (EL26-71) forces six grid operators to file different rule designs on a common deadline (Nov 16). Texas's freeze has known start dates (Aug 3, Sept 21) and no end date. This creates staggered, policy-driven variation in interconnection terms across neighboring markets while national demand drivers stay roughly common. Ember's estimate that only about 3% of capacity is shiftable and BNEF's jump in on-site data-center storage suggest firms may respond to curtailment terms by buying flexibility on-site rather than shifting load.
DataFERC eLibrary compliance filings in EL26-71, coded for curtailment terms, size thresholds and cost allocation; LBNL Queued Up and utility/ERCOT large-load interconnection reports (entries, withdrawals, MW, county); EIA-860 and 860M for planned co-located battery and gas units; EIA-930 hourly load to compute curtailment exposure under each rule.
IdentificationDifference-in-differences or event study at the region-quarter level, using the timing and type of each grid operator's adopted rule, with a border-county design for siting leakage (e.g., counties near ERCOT versus SPP). An analytical complement: a developer chooses between firm service later and interruptible service now, then decides on behind-the-meter capacity. Main threat: rule design is endogenous to queue pressure, since operators with the most speculative load may adopt the strictest rules. Gas turbine lead times (around 2031) and battery tariffs are concurrent shocks that also move behind-the-meter choices. Use pre-period queue composition as a control and test for pre-trends.
LiteratureLumpy Capacity Investment and Disinvestment Dynamics and Capacity Investment with Demand Learning provide the real-options lens on an open-ended freeze versus conditional access. A Review of Flexible Processes and Operations frames curtailable service as a flexibility contract. The Value of Coordination in Multimarket Bidding of Grid Energy Storage informs the value of behind-the-meter storage once the load is curtailable.

Idea 2

Segregation or leakage: EUDR traceability and Brazilian commodity flows

QuestionDoes the EUDR's plot-level traceability mandate cause supply chains to segregate, so that compliant, low-deforestation municipalities keep serving the EU while high-risk origins are redirected to non-EU buyers, rather than reducing deforestation? Does Argentine soymeal displace Brazil's EU share by diverting volumes away from Southeast Asia?
Why nowEnforcement begins Dec 30 on a known date, with country risk tiers creating sharp thresholds. FEFAC and Argentine crushers have publicly quantified expected displacement (4–6M t of soymeal). Brazil's political continuity after Lula's re-election holds domestic enforcement roughly constant, so the EU rule is the main shift. Timber checks start the same month, giving a second commodity.
DataUN Comtrade and Eurostat Comext monthly flows (HS 2304 soymeal, HS 44 wood) by origin and destination; Trase municipality-to-exporter-to-destination mappings; INPE PRODES deforestation by municipality; FEFAC soy risk assessments and the EUDR country benchmarking list.
IdentificationMunicipality-level difference-in-differences: pre-period EU export share multiplied by deforestation risk, interacted with the post-December-2026 period, with outcomes being the EU share of exports and total exports (to separate redirection from contraction). For Argentina, a triple difference on destination (EU versus Southeast Asia). Main threat: anticipatory front-loading before Dec 30 and the possibility of yet another enforcement delay, which would blur the timing. Harvest, price and exchange-rate shocks also move flows. Use non-EUDR commodities from the same municipalities as a placebo.
LiteratureNavigating traceability: How pricing and responsibility sharing impact quality and welfare models how traceability costs are shared, which here rise with distance from ports. Sustainability in multi-tier supply chains: Understanding the double agency role of the first-tier supplier fits traders acting as intermediaries for EU importers. Toward 'Omniscient' supply chains: Enabling and incentivizing sustainable operations frames whether visibility improves practices or simply sorts suppliers.

Idea 3

Hourly matching mandates and the optimal clean-power procurement portfolio

QuestionHow does moving from annual to hourly renewable matching (Spain's 80% hourly floor, the GHG Protocol's optional Method 1) change a large buyer's least-cost mix of solar, wind, storage, long-term PPAs and spot purchases? Which firms adopt the stricter standard voluntarily when it is optional?
Why nowThe same week brought a binding hourly mandate (Spain, with a solar exemption from the build window and a five-year transition) and a weakened voluntary standard (the GHG Protocol leaves the mandatory date open but requires firms to disclose their method). Firms told the consultation that hourly rules would push them from long-term contracts toward spot purchases (72%), a testable claim. The method disclosure creates an observable self-selection signal from 2027.
DataREE and ENTSO-E hourly Spanish generation and demand; EIA-930 hourly US grid data to build counterfactual hourly-matched emissions; CDP climate responses (location- versus market-based Scope 2, instruments used); SEC EDGAR PPA disclosures; GHG Protocol consultation respondent lists; Spanish data-center pipeline trackers to check for bunching of project sizes below 10 MW.
IdentificationAnalytical core: a stochastic portfolio model with an hourly coverage constraint, calibrated to Spanish hourly profiles, comparing the 80% floor, Malaysia-style capacity- versus consumption-based mandates, and annual matching. Empirical complement: a selection model of early Method 1 adoption on the pre-existing gap between location- and market-based Scope 2 and on grid mix; for Spain, a test for bunching of project sizes at the 10 MW threshold. Main threat: voluntary adopters are those with already-clean regional grids or existing 24/7 contracts, so adoption reflects low cost rather than commitment. Results should be read as a selection pattern, not a causal effect.
LiteratureMeeting Corporate Renewable Power Targets supplies the contract-portfolio baseline, which hourly constraints extend. The Unintended Carbon Impacts of Large-Scale Electricity Storage cautions that storage built to meet the floor may not cut emissions. Market Reaction to Mandatory Nonfinancial Disclosure informs how markets might price disclosure of which method a firm uses.

Patterns this week

One shock, many large-load rulebooks

Within one week, regulators answered the same problem (lumpy, uncertain data-center load arriving faster than grid capacity) with very different tools. Spain set an 80% hourly renewable floor with a 10 MW threshold. Malaysia approved a 30% renewable mandate. CAISO approved curtailable interim interconnection. Texas imposed an open-ended hookup freeze. In Virginia, transmission siting overrides host communities. This cross-sectional variation in rule design, much of it triggered by a single FERC order, is unusually well suited to siting, queue and investment-timing studies, and Ember's finding that only about 3% of capacity is shiftable bounds how much flexibility-based rules can deliver.

Irreversible capacity commitments under volatile forecasts

Firms across the AI-energy chain are locking in long-lived capacity well before demand is known. Anthropic disclosed $265bn in fixed commitments, gas turbine slots are booked out to about 2031, and BNEF revised its 10-year storage forecast up 30% in six months. POSCO converted a high-nickel cathode line to LFP on the strength of MOUs, and Valley Link commits 765-kV lines on the basis of operators' load forecasts. Taken together these are textbook capacity-investment-under-uncertainty settings with observable contract terms (take-or-pay, leases, MOUs) and a live risk of phantom orders, so who bears capacity risk can be measured from filings.

Fights over measurement granularity in sustainability claims

Several items turn on whether claims are measured at a fine or coarse grain. The GHG Protocol backed off mandatory hourly matching after only 12% of companies supported it, while Spain kept an hourly floor. VesselBot shows that lane-average shipping factors hide a 20–35% gap between carriers. Meta's cement certificates separate the environmental attribute from the physical product, and RepRisk shows greenwashing scrutiny moving toward transition sectors. On the policy side, the OCC's push to exempt national banks from California's SB 253/261 shows coverage itself is contested. The common OM question is how measurement rules change procurement decisions: PPA portfolios, carrier selection and offtake contracts.

Circular economy: collection and feedstock control are the binding constraints

In each circular-economy item, processing technology mattered less than getting material back. Türkiye's food-waste plants run at 48% utilization. Shaw depends on landfill tipping fees and demolition contractors to get carpet tile back, BMW's recycled-content target depends on young-fleet return lags, and VW's Zwickau hub dismantles only 4,100 vehicles a year. Clarios' purchase of Ecobat's lead smelters shows OEMs integrating backward to secure feedstock. For the advisor's agenda this points to reverse-logistics network design and vertical integration, rather than recycling technology itself, as the active research margin.
