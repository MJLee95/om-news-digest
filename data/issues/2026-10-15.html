Thursday, October 15, 2026

Today's issue covers two regulatory deadlines and three operational stress points. 01 · ESG & Disclosure

California finalizes SB 253 rules, setting November 10, 2026 as the first deadline for corporate GHG reports

ESG Today · Oct 15 · Read · ☆ Star

California's Office of Administrative Law approved CARB's initial corporate GHG reporting regulation on Oct. 12, 2026, effective immediately, leaving companies about a month to file. Firms with more than $1B in revenue doing business in California must report Scope 1 and 2 emissions by Nov. 10, 2026, and Scope 3 begins in 2027. In year one, CARB will accept reports without third-party assurance and lets firms use emissions data they have already collected. CARB's preliminary list puts more than 4,000 companies likely in scope. Enforcement of SB 261, the climate-risk reporting law for firms above $500M in revenue, remains paused under a Ninth Circuit injunction.

OM angleThis is a sharp, dated compliance shock with a published list of likely-covered firms, which makes it suitable for difference-in-differences designs. It also previews how Scope 3 requirements starting in 2027 will push emissions-data requests down supply chains.
Research QDid firms newly covered by SB 253 change how they measure and report Scope 1 and 2 emissions relative to similar uncovered firms? Did their suppliers start receiving, and answering, more emissions-data requests?
Data / IDTreatment: firms on CARB's preliminary in-scope list. Controls: firms just below the $1B revenue threshold, or firms with little California activity. Outcomes: changes in CDP responses (disclosure, assurance, supply chain module requests), with EPA GHGRP facility emissions as a check on actual emissions. Supplier links can come from FactSet Revere or, as a public alternative, Compustat customer segments. Shock timing: Feb. 26 board approval, June 24 deadline extension and Oct. 12 OAL approval, which together allow an event study using SEC EDGAR filings.
Keywordsmandatory climate reporting · scope 3 disclosure · supplier emissions · state environmental policy

02 · Energy & AI Data Centers

PJM to study SoftBank's ~10-GW Ohio AI campus and its gas generation and 765-kV lines under the state agreement approach

Utility Dive · Oct 14 · Read · ☆ Star

PJM has filed a study agreement at FERC to evaluate SoftBank's 'Flagship' AI data center complex in Ohio, along with the generation and transmission needed to serve it, under its state agreement approach. An AEP Ohio data center tariff will serve an initial 800-MW phase. The PJM study covers another 9.6 GW of load plus 10.2 GW of new gas generation that SB Energy would build in phases through 2032. The February announcement had instead cited about $33B for 9.2 GW of gas. AEP Ohio plans three 765-kV lines, and most of the project would sit at DOE's former Portsmouth uranium enrichment site in Pike County.

OM angleThe project bundles large-load interconnection, co-located generation and policy-driven transmission planning into a single case. It raises questions of capacity sequencing and of who bears the cost of grid expansion for one customer.
Research QHow does planning large data center loads together with dedicated generation under PJM's state agreement approach change interconnection timelines and transmission cost allocation, compared with the standard interconnection queue?
Data / IDUnit of analysis: large-load and generation projects. Compare timelines, milestones and cost-allocation terms in FERC eLibrary filings for state-agreement projects with standard queue projects tracked in LBNL Queued Up data. AEP Ohio data center tariff dockets on the Ohio PUC docket system give the phased load ramp. PJM capacity auction results can show zonal price effects.
Keywordsinterconnection policy · data center electricity demand · interconnection queues · federal-state jurisdiction · capacity investment under uncertainty
03 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Greek draft law would require 60% renewable power for data centers, a €200k/MW grid-queue guarantee and a public registry

DatacenterDynamics · Oct 15 · Read · ☆ Star

Greece's energy ministry has drafted a law requiring data centers to source at least 60% of their electricity from renewables, met through direct connection to renewables or storage or through PPAs. Sources disagree on whether the rule covers only new sites. Developers would post a €200,000/MW bank guarantee to reserve grid capacity, could not split projects to get lighter rules, and could be offered flexible, capped connections where the grid is constrained. Grid operators would publish regional connection capacity every quarter, and the regulator RAAEY would create a data center registry. Public comment runs until Oct. 24, 2026.

OM angleThe bill combines a green procurement mandate with queue-screening collateral and capacity caps, which are policy levers OM models of siting and procurement can evaluate. If enacted, it would also generate new public data on capacity and registered facilities.
Research QHow does a per-MW collateral requirement combined with a 60% renewable sourcing mandate affect the volume of speculative data center connection requests and where data centers choose to locate?
Data / IDIf the bill passes, compare connection applications and withdrawals before and after the €200k/MW guarantee, using IPTO's quarterly regional capacity publications and the RAAEY registry. Use other EU countries without such rules as a control group. Model procurement choice (PPA versus co-located storage) under the 60% target.
Keywordscollateral requirements · capacity caps · facility siting · interconnection queues · data center electricity demand

04 · Circular Economy

Circular Action Alliance cuts first-year California SB 54 EPR budget to about $1.1B in revised plan; CEO to depart

Waste Dive · Oct 14 · Read · ☆ Star

Circular Action Alliance submitted a revised 495-page SB 54 packaging EPR plan to CalRecycle on Oct. 13, 2026, after a public comment period that drew more than 2,400 comments. The 2027 budget falls from up to $1.87B to about $1.11B, and the five-year range falls from $9.35B–$17.2B to $7.81B–$13.1B. The 2027 budget includes a $500M mitigation fund and a MRF capital fund capped at $100M, and the plan concedes that even PET and HDPE bottles fall short of the first plastic recycling target. CalRecycle has 75 days to act, and founding CEO Jeff Fielkow will leave later this year, with Jeff Meyers as interim CEO.

OM angleThe plan offers a rare line-item view of a PRO's cost structure, its fee-setting logic and its investments in recycling infrastructure under the largest US packaging EPR program.
Research QHow do producer fees and MRF capital allocations in SB 54's program plan relate to material-level recycling gaps? Do these fee structures create incentives for packaging redesign?
Data / IDCode the plan's budget lines and fee methodology by material from CalRecycle's SB 54 records. Compare with Oregon and Colorado PRO plans using state EPR reports, and use EPA recycling rates by material as a benchmark. Track packaging changes by covered brands over time with Mintel GNPD.

05 · Trade & Industrial Policy

CBP proposes requiring filing by arrival for imports of $2,500 or less, plus a new postal entry type with 15-day abandonment

Supply Chain Dive · Oct 14 · Read · ☆ Star

CBP's proposed rule, published Oct. 8, 2026, would require electronic Type 11 informal entries to be filed on or before arrival, instead of within 15 days after. CBP's own sample of September–November 2025 entries found more than half would have missed that deadline. A new Entry Type 13 would cover mail shipments of $2,500 or less, with tracking numbers placed on manifests. Mail shipments not filed within 15 days would be deemed abandoned, compared with USPS's current 30-day hold. CBP says it cannot certify that the rule avoids significant impacts on small businesses, and comments are due Dec. 7, 2026.

OM angleTightening filing deadlines turns customs compliance into a binding operational constraint for cross-border e-commerce logistics and postal capacity.
Research QHow would moving informal entries to filing-before-arrival change clearance times, abandonment rates and the split of low-value imports between postal and express channels?
Data / IDUse the proposal's Federal Register docket and comments to gauge expected burden. Track Census trade data on low-value and postal-channel imports before and after the final rule's effective date. If accessible, Panjiva bill-of-lading data can show shifts in shipment consolidation.

Thread to pull

Greece's €200k/MW guarantee and PJM's state agreement approach are two different tools for rationing scarce grid capacity among very large data center loads. A comparative study could ask which design, collateral screening or state-sponsored co-planning, better filters out speculative requests and speeds real capacity onto the grid. The study would combine FERC filings and LBNL queue data with the public registry and quarterly capacity data the Greek draft would create if enacted.
