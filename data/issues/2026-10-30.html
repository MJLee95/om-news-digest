01 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Ember: only about 3% of global data-centre capacity can shift power demand by 2030; onsite generation is a much bigger lever

Latitude Media · Oct 29 · Read · ☆ Star

On 29 Oct 2026, think tank Ember estimated that about 3% of global data-centre capacity (not 3% of data centres) could take part in load shifting by 2030. The reason is that about 87% of 2030 capacity is expected to run cloud, enterprise IT and AI inference, which must respond to users in real time. Across nine markets, load shifting cuts peak demand by only about 0.1–0.6% in seven, and by about 1.5% in Ireland and Malaysia, where data centres could reach roughly a quarter of peak demand.

BackgroundGrid planners and data-centre developers increasingly cite flexibility as a way to connect large AI loads faster without building new peak capacity. AI training is the flexible workload, but it is the smallest share of capacity. In a Texas case study, two large sites (Stargate Abilene and Comanche Circle) could cut up to 2.7 GW of peak grid demand by switching to onsite generation, versus about 0.55 GW from load shifting. Ember calls its numbers rough estimates based on limited data and published an interactive calculator so readers can test other assumptions.
ProblemIf most data-centre load is effectively inflexible, flexibility-based connection deals and demand-response assumptions may overstate how much grid capacity can be avoided. The alternative lever, onsite generation, is often gas and risks fossil lock-in. Malaysia's pairing of data centres with large solar and battery projects is the counterexample. And if planners ignore onsite generation that already exists, two sets of power plants could be built for the same demand, with consumers paying for both.
Where & whyThe friction sits at the interface between grid capacity planning and the data-centre workload mix. Latency-sensitive inference and cloud workloads have no slack in time. Planners also lack information about operators' onsite assets and true flexibility, which creates both information asymmetry and a coordination failure between utility resource plans and private behind-the-meter investment.
Stakeholders
Grid operators and utility planners (e.g., ERCOT, EirGrid) — Need accurate peak-load forecasts; risk building duplicate capacity or under-planning reliability
Data-centre operators and hyperscalers — Want fast interconnection; must choose between flexibility commitments, onsite gas, or clean onsite generation and storage
Electricity consumers — May pay for duplicated generation if onsite assets are ignored in planning
Clean-energy and battery developers — Stand to gain if onsite solar and storage replace gas as the backup lever
Research hints
💡 Treat flexibility as a portfolio problem: workload mix (training vs inference) determines the shiftable fraction, which in turn sets the value of onsite storage versus generation.
💡 Puzzle: why does flexibility matter far more in Ireland and Malaysia than in Texas? Data-centre share of peak demand looks like the key moderator.
💡 Planning under private information: model a utility that cannot observe a large load's onsite generation and must decide how much capacity to build.
Data leadsEmber's interactive flexibility calculator; EIA-930 hourly demand; ERCOT large-load interconnection reports; EIA-860 for onsite and behind-the-meter gas units near data-centre sites.
Keywordsdata center electricity demand · demand response · interruptible load · load forecasting · gas turbines
Related research
A Review of Flexible Processes and Operations (Production and Operations Management, 2019)
Reviews process flexibility and when partial flexibility captures most of its value; this frames how much a 3% shiftable share can actually buy a grid.
Online Rack Placement in Large-Scale Data Centers: Online Sampling Optimization and Deployment (Operations Research, 2026)
Optimizes rack placement under power and cooling constraints inside large data centres, the facility-level counterpart to grid-level load management.
02 · Critical Minerals & Batteries

Hungary's post-Orban government halts or restricts battery plants, unsettling Europe's EV battery hub

Automotive News · Oct 30 · Read · ☆ Star

Under new Prime Minister Peter Magyar, elected in April 2026, Hungary has tightened environmental oversight of the battery plants that Viktor Orban courted. Semcorp's separator factory in Debrecen is entering its fifth month of shutdown after contamination was found in tests; Semcorp makes a reported 40% of the world's separators. CATL's €7.34bn, 100 GWh Debrecen project had cell production paused over workers' nickel exposure, and on 16 Aug 2026 the government said key investment projects would no longer be exempt from environmental and planning rules.

BackgroundUnder Orban, Hungary built itself into a European battery hub by attracting Chinese and South Korean component makers, often with exemptions from planning and environmental rules. Magyar's Tisza party won in a landslide after promising a tougher line on the sector. Environment Minister Laszlo Gajdos threatened closures for plants that break the rules and promised what he called Europe's strictest pollution fines. A new top pollution authority was proposed, though its launch is unconfirmed. CATL's 5 GWh module plant started in May 2026. Nine CATL employees were found in August to have elevated nickel levels, and the first cell phase received a production licence on 25 Aug 2026. The government also said in May it would not support expanding CATL Debrecen beyond the first plant.
ProblemHost-country support has reversed for capacity that is already sunk. Firms must now decide whether to remediate and stay, scale back planned phases, or relocate. Downstream carmakers face supply risk for critical components, including a separator supplier with a large global share. Whether CATL's cell production has restarted is not confirmed.
Where & whyThe shock hits the cell and component manufacturing tier, where capacity is lumpy and site-specific. Siting choices made under one political regime became exposed to regulatory and political risk when local environmental concerns turned into a national political fight. Concentrated suppliers (Semcorp's separators) make a single-site enforcement action a supply-chain-wide risk.
Stakeholders
Semcorp, CATL, Samsung, SK On — Sunk plant investments; possible fines, licence delays and capped expansion
European carmakers relying on Hungarian supply — Risk of shortages of separators, cells and modules, and cost of qualifying alternative suppliers
Hungarian government (Magyar, Gajdos) — Delivering on environmental promises while retaining jobs and investment
Debrecen residents and workers — Groundwater and soil safety, occupational nickel exposure, local employment
Research hints
💡 Real-options lens: how does a political regime change alter the value of the option to expand or abandon a phased battery plant?
💡 Natural experiment: compare announced capacity, expansion phases and supplier-qualification moves by firms with Hungarian versus other EU battery sites before and after April 2026.
💡 Supplier concentration: what does a months-long outage at a separator maker with a reported 40% global share reveal about substitutability in battery bills of materials?
Data leadsPlant-level capacity trackers (Benchmark/BNEF; public alternative: company announcements and press coverage); UN Comtrade Hungarian battery and separator exports; GDELT for local protest and enforcement events.
Keywordsregulatory uncertainty · facility siting · supplier concentration · regulatory enforcement · capacity investment under uncertainty
Related research
Global operations and supply‐chain management under the political economy (Journal of Operations Management, 2022)
Frames global operations decisions in terms of political economy, the lens needed for a host-government policy reversal.
Political Uncertainty and the Timing of Mass Layoffs (Production and Operations Management, 2025)
Shows how political uncertainty around elections shifts the timing of plant closures and layoffs under a real-options logic.
03 · Supply Chain Resilience

Kroger targets more than 10% direct sourcing, up from under 1%, with owned import hubs in South America and Asia

Supply Chain Dive · Oct 29 · Read · ☆ Star

At its 20 Oct 2026 investor day, Kroger CFO David Kennerley said the grocer aims to source more than 10% of goods directly from producers, up from under 1% today; the target year was not confirmed in the notes. Kroger is launching sourcing hubs in South America and Asia toward a 'fully owned import distribution model over time,' with its first international hire in place in South America and Asia expansion planned for 2027. About 40% of its imported grapes for the November 2026 season were bought directly from growers in Peru.

BackgroundCEO Greg Foran set out a cost-of-goods strategy in June 2026, built on supplier negotiations and direct sourcing. Other outlets report a target of about $4bn in annualized savings by 2029, with cost of goods about half of that. Kroger is also working on direct sourcing of citrus, shrimp and frozen vegetables, and says it is using better data to support buyers in supplier negotiations.
ProblemCutting out importers and intermediaries captures their margin and gives Kroger more visibility. In exchange, Kroger takes on quality, phytosanitary, logistics and seasonal-volume risk that intermediaries used to pool across many buyers. The question is whether vertical integration of import and distribution improves cost and resilience, or concentrates risk for perishables.
Where & whyThe change happens at the import-intermediary link in fresh and frozen food chains. The trade-offs include double marginalization versus intermediaries' scale and risk pooling, information asymmetry about grower quality and volumes, and the need for owned cold-chain and import capability in each sourcing region.
Stakeholders
Kroger — Lower cost of goods and better visibility, at the price of fixed overseas capability and new operational risk
Importers and distributors — Lose margin and volume as Kroger bypasses them
Growers in Peru and elsewhere — Possible higher prices and stable demand, but bargaining with a single powerful buyer
Competing grocers and CPG suppliers — Pressure to match Kroger's cost structure and negotiation tactics
Research hints
💡 Make-or-buy with risk pooling: when does an intermediary that pools many retailers' seasonal produce demand beat direct sourcing?
💡 Test for shifts in Kroger's consignee and shipper patterns in import records before and after the strategy, product by product (grapes, citrus, shrimp).
Data leadsBill-of-lading data (Panjiva/ImportGenius; public alternative: Census USA Trade Online by HS code and district); USDA AMS Market News grape and citrus shipments and prices; Kroger 10-K and investor-day transcript.
Keywordsvertical integration · seasonal sourcing · produce supply chains · cold chain · supplier diversification
Related research
Production Chain Organization in the Digital Age: Information Technology Use and Vertical Integration in U.S. Manufacturing (Management Science, 2024)
Studies how IT access changes vertical integration in production chains, relevant to Kroger's data-enabled move toward owned sourcing.
Supply-Side Story: Risks, Guarantees, Competition, and Information Asymmetry (Management Science, 2012)
Models a buyer choosing between cheaper, riskier suppliers and more reliable ones under information asymmetry, the trade-off in bypassing intermediaries.
04 · Circular Economy

BMW reports one-third recycled content and targets at least 50% by 2030, betting on direct battery-cell recycling

Recycling International · Oct 29 · Read · ☆ Star

According to IndexBox relaying Recycling International, BMW told a Berlin audience it has reached one-third recycled content in its vehicles and aims for at least 50% by 2030; what the one-third covers is unclear. BMW's €10m Cell Recycling Competence Centre in Salching, Bavaria, opened in 2025. It develops 'direct recycling' that returns lithium, cobalt, graphite and nickel to battery production without energy-heavy heat or chemical processing.

BackgroundBMW has delivered 2 million battery-electric vehicles, and EVs were one in four of its sales this year. It has discussed a 50% recycled-content goal since around 2023 (the Car2Car project), so the target is not new. The centre works on production scrap and whole cells: BMW takes batteries apart, separates the layers and is trialling dry-powder systems. It partners with Encory, its joint venture with Interzero. A BMW representative said about 70% of value currently leaves Europe through battery-material flows.
ProblemMeeting a 50% recycled-content target requires secure secondary-material supply. Over the near term, though, the main feedstock is production scrap, because the EV fleet is young and few end-of-life packs are returning. BMW also has to decide whether to build in-house direct-recycling capability or rely on external recyclers and the material flows that currently leave Europe.
Where & whyThe friction is in reverse logistics and material recovery. Return flows of end-of-life batteries are uncertain and lagged, and direct recycling is not yet at scale. Value leaks out of Europe through material flows sent abroad for processing, a combination of capacity shortfall and raw-material security concerns.
Stakeholders
BMW — Hitting recycled-content targets and capturing material value while securing raw materials
Encory/Interzero and other recyclers — Feedstock volumes and the business case for European processing capacity
EU policymakers — Keeping battery-material value and processing within Europe
Cell makers and material refiners — Demand for, and competition from, closed-loop secondary materials
Research hints
💡 Closed-loop supply models with stochastic, lagged returns: how does EV fleet age limit achievable recycled content compared with relying on production scrap?
💡 Make-or-buy for recycling capability: compare OEMs with in-house recycling centres to those using contracts with independent recyclers.
Data leadsBMW Group annual and sustainability reports (recycled-content disclosures); Benchmark/BNEF recycling capacity data (public alternative: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries for lithium, cobalt and nickel); Eurostat end-of-life vehicle statistics.
Keywordsbattery recycling · secondary materials · closed-loop supply chain · raw material security · reverse logistics
Related research
LOOKING FORWARD, PUSHING BACK AND PEERING SIDEWAYS: ANALYZING THE SUSTAINABILITY OF INDUSTRIAL SYMBIOSIS (Journal of Supply Chain Management, 2009)
Compares industrial symbiosis with conventional supply chains for reusing byproducts and end-of-life products, relevant to recycling production scrap back into cells.
05 · Logistics Decarbonization

VesselBot: carrier choice changes well-to-wake emissions intensity by up to 35% on the same container trade

Hellenic Shipping News · Oct 29 · Read · ☆ Star

VesselBot's Q3 2026 report covers 83,817 voyages by 6,730 container vessels. It finds a 20–35% gap in well-to-wake emissions intensity between the most and least efficient carriers on four major fronthaul trades. Fleet average intensity was 235.1 g CO₂e per TEU-km (up 0.5% year on year), while total emissions fell 1.9% to 55.5m t and cargo moved rose 1.2% to 712bn TEU-km.

BackgroundThe analysis combines AIS tracking with digital models of individual vessels. It is a vendor's own analysis and has not been independently checked. On direct China/East Asia–Northern Europe sailings, Evergreen had the highest emissions per voyage (14,273 t) but the lowest intensity (47.4 g, about 17% below the 57.3 g trade average), with 20,400 TEU ships at 96% utilization. Voyages under 50 g averaged 80% full, against 32% for voyages at 800 g or more. Feeder ships make up 65% of voyages and worsened 1.3% to 285.9 g, while five of six size classes improved by 3.4–12.9%. Port time rose 6.2–27.3% across size classes, and Cape routings kept Asia–Northern Europe voyages at 33.2 days despite 166 Suez transits (versus 16 a year earlier).
ProblemShippers reporting Scope 3 emissions usually rely on lane-average default factors, which hide large carrier-level differences. That weakens the incentive to choose lower-emission carriers. The ranking also depends on the metric: Evergreen is the worst per voyage but the best per TEU-km.
Where & whyThe friction is in shippers' carrier selection and emissions accounting. Shippers cannot observe the actual efficiency of their voyages, so information asymmetry is the core issue. Utilization, vessel size and network design (feeders, Cape routings, congested ports) drive intensity more than technology does, and procurement contracts rarely price these factors in.
Stakeholders
Shippers and beneficial cargo owners — Accurate Scope 3 reporting and a credible basis for green carrier procurement
Container carriers (e.g., Evergreen, Hapag-Lloyd, MSC) — Competitive position if intensity rankings influence contract awards
Emissions data vendors (VesselBot) — Market for primary-data emissions accounting
Regulators and standard setters — Defining acceptable emissions factors for disclosure regimes
Research hints
💡 Metric choice as a puzzle: per-voyage versus per-TEU-km rankings diverge, and utilization drives most of the gap. Which metric should a procurement contract reward?
💡 Green procurement auction: model a shipper that weights carrier bids by emissions intensity, and ask whether this induces carriers to raise utilization or simply re-sort cargo across lanes.
Data leadsNOAA MarineCadastre AIS for US waters; CDP supply-chain responses for shippers' reported logistics emissions; Sea-Intelligence/Xeneta schedule and rate data (commercial).
Keywordsscope 3 disclosure · capacity utilization · market-based emissions accounting · procurement auctions · chokepoint disruption
Related research
Vessel sharing and its impact on maritime operations and carbon emissions (Production and Operations Management, 2022)
Quantifies how vessel-sharing coordination changes container-ship utilization and carbon emissions, the mechanism behind VesselBot's utilization–intensity gap.
Thread to pull

Three of today's items turn on utilization and flexibility as hidden parameters. Only about 3% of data-centre capacity is shiftable, container emissions intensity tracks load factor more than carrier identity, and BMW's recycled content is limited by when and how many end-of-life packs come back. Kroger's direct sourcing and Hungary's policy reversal both raise the question of who bears the risk once an intermediary or host-government buffer disappears. Comparing these cases could reveal a common structure for when firms internalize risk and capacity versus contracting them out.

New in the journals

EXPRESS: The Role of Ease-of-Return Experience on Contingent Free Shipping Policies
Production and Operations Management

The paper studies how a retailer should set the threshold and fee of a contingent free-shipping policy when customers pad orders to qualify and later return the extra items. The value of the policy depends on how easy returns are, which shifts the balance between larger orders and larger returns.

Return-swelling is a hidden reverse-logistics and emissions cost, linking retail policy design to the circular-economy and freight-decarbonization themes in today's BMW and VesselBot items.
