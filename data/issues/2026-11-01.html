Daily Clippings

Sunday, November 1, 2026

Today's issue covers five policy and operating shocks: a Texas freeze on data center grid hookups and permits that is cooling dealmaking, an FCC ban on Chinese testing labs that targets a little-noticed certification step, EU deforestation rules that may shift soymeal sourcing from Brazil to Argentina, a non-Chinese LFP cathode supplier locking in all three Korean cell makers, and a 3,000-truck middle-mile electrification deal in India.

01 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Texas M&A deal count and value both fell in Q3 2026 as Abbott's data center hookup and permit freeze weighs on dealmaking

Yahoo Finance · Oct 31 · Read · ☆ Star

The Texas Lawbook (Allen Pusey, Oct 26, 2026) reports that Texas M&A deal count and deal value both fell in Q3 2026. It calls this the first joint decline since 2025, and dealmakers blame Gov. Greg Abbott's pause on data center grid connections and permits. Some investors, including those with behind-the-meter power, are using the pause to make sure their large power-infrastructure investments will pay off. The exact Q3 figures are paywalled.

BackgroundOn Aug 3, 2026, Abbott halted data center approvals pending audits. He asked the PUC and ERCOT to require developers to disclose tax breaks, power and water use, community impacts and who owns each facility, and said about 90% of new power requests come from data centers. On Sept 21 he ordered TCEQ to halt all data-center-related permits until ERCOT finishes auditing its waitlist of data centers seeking grid connections. BloombergNEF (via CNBC) estimates almost 20% of the 253-GW US data center pipeline, nearly 50 GW, is at risk, with possibly more than $8B in lost data center revenue by Q1 2027. Data centers have also become an issue in the 2026 midterms, including Abbott's reelection race.
ProblemTexas is pausing large-load growth to audit queue quality and resource use while investors face an open-ended halt with no announced end date. The open question is how much the freeze, as opposed to the election, rising bond yields, inflation, geopolitical turmoil and higher borrowing costs, explains the slowdown. A related question is whether the pause pushes projects toward self-supply or out of state.
Where & whyThe friction sits at the interconnection and permitting gate, where speculative large-load requests crowd the ERCOT queue and the state lacks information on which projects are real. The audit-and-disclose response addresses that information asymmetry but creates regulatory uncertainty about timing. That uncertainty raises the option value of waiting on irreversible, lumpy capacity investments. Behind-the-meter developers partly bypass the grid gate, which may create a selection effect in who keeps investing.
Stakeholders
ERCOT and the Texas PUC — Want to screen speculative load requests and protect grid reliability without choking off investment.
Data center developers and hyperscalers — Face delayed energization and stranded capital; may relocate or self-generate.
Behind-the-meter power investors — Could gain a relative advantage if grid hookups stay frozen, but need assurance their investments will pay off.
Gov. Abbott and Texas voters — Weigh economic development against grid strain, water use and community impacts in an election year.
M&A advisors and dealmakers — Lose deal flow while the timing of the audits remains uncertain.
Research hints
💡 Real-options models of capacity investment under regulatory uncertainty: what does an open-ended pause with an unknown end date do to investment timing compared with a fixed moratorium?
💡 Compare queue entries and withdrawals in ERCOT with those in neighboring markets such as PJM or SPP before and after Aug 3 and Sept 21 to look for siting leakage.
💡 Puzzle: the Lawbook's week ended Aug 15 showed energy-infrastructure deals continuing (15 deals, about $11.3B) even as overall M&A later slowed. Which segments are insulated, and why?
Data leadsERCOT large-load interconnection reports and LBNL queue data for queue dynamics, Texas PUC dockets for the disclosure requirements, and SEC EDGAR 8-Ks for deal announcements.
Keywordssiting moratorium · interconnection queues · regulatory uncertainty · capacity investment under uncertainty · data center electricity demand
Related research
Lumpy Capacity Investment and Disinvestment Dynamics (Operations Research, 2010)
Models lumpy capacity addition and withdrawal in oligopoly, including preemption races, which is relevant to how data center developers race or wait when interconnection capacity is gated.
Capacity Investment with Demand Learning (Operations Research, 2016)
Covers capacity investment while learning about demand, analogous to investors waiting for ERCOT's audit to resolve uncertainty before committing.
02 · Trade & Industrial Policy

China vows countermeasures after FCC bars Chinese labs from certifying US-bound electronics starting December 2028

South China Morning Post · Oct 31 · Read · ☆ Star

On Oct 29, 2026, the FCC voted to bar labs in China, and in other economies without a mutual-recognition or reciprocal trade agreement with the US, from testing electronics such as smartphones and computers for US certification, effective December 2028. On Oct 30, China's Ministry of Commerce said the move "seriously disrupted the stability of global industrial and supply chains" and warned of countermeasures if the US persists in acting unilaterally. It also said it remains open to talks.

BackgroundSources differ on how much US-bound electronics testing happens in China: SCMP, citing the FCC, says more than 80%, while Asia Times, citing STDaily, says about 75%. China had 175 FCC-recognized labs as of Sept 8. Asia Times reports a basic test costs about US$400–1,300 in China versus US$3,000–4,000 in a US lab. The FCC cites alleged ties between the targeted labs and the PLA. In July 2026 the FCC banned imports of new Chinese robots and power inverters, and in August China retaliated in a separate dispute by tightening drone export controls and sanctioning US testing firms. China says it proposed a bilateral mutual-recognition agreement, but the US was "extremely passive."
ProblemConformity testing is a mandatory service step between product design and US market entry, and most of that capacity sits in China. Moving it means paying several times more per test and building lab capacity elsewhere within about two years, and the risk of retaliation and a possible mutual-recognition deal make the end state uncertain.
Where & whyThe bottleneck is the certification node, a service chokepoint that rarely appears in supply chain maps. Testing is co-located with Chinese manufacturing for cost and speed, so the ban forces a separation that can lengthen time-to-market. Accredited labs outside China may lack capacity, and firms must decide whether to pre-certify before December 2028 or shift testing elsewhere amid uncertainty over policy reversal.
Stakeholders
Electronics OEMs and contract manufacturers — Face higher testing costs, longer certification lead times and capacity scrambles.
Chinese FCC-recognized labs — Stand to lose most US certification business.
Labs in the US and mutual-recognition partner economies — Could capture demand but must expand accredited capacity quickly.
FCC / US government — Wants to address security concerns about alleged PLA-linked labs.
China's Ministry of Commerce — Seeks a mutual-recognition deal or reversal and threatens countermeasures.
Research hints
💡 Watch for pre-buy behavior: certification filings may bunch before the December 2028 effective date, similar to tariff front-running.
💡 Use queueing or capacity-expansion models of a regulated service bottleneck to study how lead times respond when demand shifts to a scarcer lab network.
💡 Compare with the July 2026 FCC ban on Chinese robots and inverters to contrast restricting a product with restricting a service node.
Data leadsThe FCC Equipment Authorization database, which records the test lab for each grant, and the FCC recognized-lab list, plus Federal Register rule texts and Panjiva bill-of-lading data (public alternative: Census trade data) for downstream trade effects.
Keywordssourcing restrictions · chokepoint disruption · pre-buy behavior · supplier switching · conformity assessment
Related research
Global operations and supply‐chain management under the political economy (Journal of Operations Management, 2022)
Frames global operations under political economy forces, the lens for a security-motivated restriction on a cross-border service link.
03 · Food & Agriculture

EU deforestation rules could shut out up to 4M tons of Brazilian soymeal, and Argentina says it can fill the gap

Business Recorder · Oct 31 · Read · ☆ Star

From Dec 30, the EUDR requires EU importers to prove products aren't linked to deforested land, with significant penalties. The European feed industry federation (FEFAC) warns that traceability requirements could shut out up to 4M tons of Brazilian soymeal, about 17% of Brazil's exports. Argentina's CIARA-CEC president Gustavo Idigoras puts Brazil's potential losses at 5–6M tons and says "we can easily fill that gap" (Reuters, Oct 28, 2026).

BackgroundBrazil supplied about half of EU soymeal imports last year and exported a record 23.3M metric tons in 2025. FEFAC rates Brazil "medium" risk, citing "structural and logistical hurdles" in Mato Grosso and other states. Argentina and Paraguay are rated "low" risk, and FEFAC projects nearly all Argentine soymeal in the first half of 2027 would comply. Argentina is the world's largest soymeal exporter, shipping about 27M tons last year, roughly a quarter to Southeast Asia.
ProblemA traceability mandate may reshuffle trade rather than reduce deforestation. Compliant origins could serve the EU while non-traceable Brazilian meal is redirected to less regulated markets. Brazilian growers argue the law unfairly targets Brazil, and the size of the shift remains contested, with estimates ranging from 4M to 6M tons.
Where & whyThe friction is upstream traceability. Farmers in central Brazil are more than 2,000 km from ports, and commingled logistics make plot-level tracing costly. The risk ratings give low-risk origins a compliance-cost advantage, which can produce segregated supply chains: compliant volumes go to the EU and the rest goes elsewhere, a classic leakage pattern.
Stakeholders
EU feed importers (FEFAC members) — Need compliant supply and want to avoid penalties and supply shortfalls.
Brazilian soy farmers (Aprosoja) — Risk losing EU market share and view the law as unfairly targeting Brazil.
Argentine crushers (CIARA-CEC) — Hope to gain EU share, possibly by diverting volumes from Southeast Asia.
Traders and logistics operators (e.g., Max Agro) — Must build traceability across long inland supply chains.
EU regulators — Want deforestation reduced rather than displaced, and must enforce on schedule.
Research hints
💡 Trade-diversion and leakage lens: track whether Brazil's soymeal shifts to non-EU destinations while Argentina's moves from Southeast Asia toward the EU.
💡 Model segregated compliant versus non-compliant supply with traceability costs that rise with distance from ports.
💡 Country risk tiers create a sharp regulatory threshold, which could be useful for comparing medium- and low-risk origins.
Data leadsUN Comtrade and Eurostat Comext monthly soymeal flows (HS 2304) by origin and destination, along with FEFAC soy risk assessments and EUDR country benchmarking.
Keywordsdeforestation · multi-tier traceability · sourcing restrictions · supplier switching · trade diversion
Related research
Navigating traceability: How pricing and responsibility sharing impact quality and welfare (Production and Operations Management, 2026)
A game-theoretic model of how traceability systems and responsibility sharing affect quality and welfare, relevant to who bears EUDR compliance costs.
Sustainability in multi‐tier supply chains: Understanding the double agency role of the first‐tier supplier (Journal of Operations Management, 2015)
Studies how first-tier suppliers act as agents enforcing sustainability compliance deeper in the chain, mirroring traders' role under EUDR.
04 · Critical Minerals & Batteries

POSCO Future M signs LFP cathode supply deals with LG Energy Solution, SK On and Samsung SDI

THE ELEC, Korea Electronics Industry Media · Oct 31 · Read · ☆ Star

On Oct 30, 2026, POSCO Future M signed an MOU with LG Energy Solution to supply 720,000 tons of cathode and anode materials, including LFP, over 2027–2033. This follows an SK On LFP cathode deal of about ₩1.07 trillion over 2027–2029 (announced Sept 22) and a Samsung SDI LFP cathode deal of about ₩6 trillion ($4.5B), disclosed apparently Oct 6. A high-nickel line at POSCO's Pohang plant will be converted to LFP, with full-scale supply starting in early 2027.

BackgroundLFP batteries are used in EVs and energy storage. Korean cell makers have historically focused on high-nickel chemistries. The LG MOU also covers artificial graphite anodes, to be made in Korea and Vietnam, and joint development of ultra-high-nickel, sodium-ion and silicon anode materials. The LG deal is a non-binding MOU covering several materials. An unconfirmed August deal for 190,000 tons of LFP to a Korean customer was also reported.
ProblemCell makers are seeking non-Chinese LFP supply. Within about a month, all three Korean cell makers committed to the same domestic cathode supplier, which concentrates supply in one firm. POSCO, in turn, is repurposing high-nickel capacity, betting on a chemistry shift.
Where & whyThe friction arises at the cathode stage, where cell makers want non-Chinese LFP sources. Few non-Chinese firms supply LFP cathode, so buyers converge on one firm. Long-term contracts and MOUs help underwrite POSCO's line conversion, but they create mutual lock-in and leave the supplier's high-nickel line idle if chemistry demand shifts again.
Stakeholders
POSCO Future M — Fills its converted lines with long-term volume but carries conversion and chemistry risk.
LG Energy Solution, SK On and Samsung SDI — Secure non-Chinese LFP supply but share one supplier with their rivals.
EV and energy storage customers — Want lower-cost LFP cells sourced outside China.
Chinese LFP cathode producers — Could lose Korean customers seeking non-Chinese sources.
Research hints
💡 Model a shared supplier serving competing buyers: how capacity allocation priority and exclusivity terms shape each cell maker's contracting.
💡 Look at stock price reactions of the three cell makers to each POSCO announcement.
💡 Puzzle: why convert a high-nickel line rather than build new capacity? A technology-switching capacity model may help.
Data leadsKorean DART disclosures for contract values and terms, plus Benchmark/BNEF battery supply chain data (public alternative: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries for lithium and graphite).
Keywordssupplier concentration · long-term supply contracts · foreign entity of concern · raw material security · battery chemistry shift
Related research
Ch‐Ch‐Ch‐Ch‐Changes: The Impact of Supply Base Growth, Contraction, and Turnover on Firm Innovation (Journal of Operations Management, 2025)
Examines how supply base growth, contraction and turnover affect firm innovation, relevant as Korean cell makers reconfigure their supplier base toward domestic LFP.
05 · Logistics Decarbonization

Amazon and Tata Motors begin deploying 3,000 electric trucks in Amazon's India middle-mile network, owned by carrier partners

Electric Cars Report · Oct 31 · Read · ☆ Star

On Oct 27, 2026, Amazon and Tata Motors announced that 3,000 Tata light- and medium-duty electric trucks will join Amazon's India middle-mile network. The companies call it the largest electric-truck deployment by an e-commerce company in India. Amazon's carrier partners will own and operate the trucks. The rollout starts in Delhi during the festive season, and the next phase targets the 2,800-km Delhi–Bengaluru–Chennai NH48 corridor across seven states, with charging hubs built with Yahhvi.

BackgroundAmazon already runs more than 15,000 EVs in 500+ Indian cities and targets net-zero carbon by 2040. Tata Motors will provide charging infrastructure, servicing and operational support, and Automotive World says the trucks include the Ultra E.7. Delhi's 2026 EV Policy lets the first 1,000 privately owned medium-duty e-trucks registered within three months of launch enter the city at any hour for 10 years. The deal value, the full deployment timeline, the light/medium split and the timing of the NH48 phase are not disclosed.
ProblemElectrifying middle-mile freight requires corridor charging and upfront capital. Amazon is pushing adoption through carriers that bear the ownership and utilization risk, while Tata bundles vehicles with charging and service. Whether the economics hold on long intercity routes remains open.
Where & whyThe friction sits in the middle-mile link and in the split of ownership between Amazon and its carriers. Carriers own the assets while Amazon claims the emissions goal, so incentives may not align. Corridor charging is a coordination problem, which explains why OEM, charging partner and shipper are investing together. Delhi's time-access exemption, capped at the first 1,000 trucks, adds an operating benefit that may favor early registration.
Stakeholders
Amazon India — Wants to cut emissions toward its 2040 net-zero goal without owning trucks.
Carrier partners — Bear EV capital costs, charging downtime and utilization risk.
Tata Motors — Gains volume and sells a vehicle, charging and service bundle.
Yahhvi (charging hubs) — Builds corridor charging and needs utilization to recover investment.
Delhi and Indian governments — Want cleaner urban freight and use access rules as incentives.
Research hints
💡 Delhi's capped any-hour access rule for the first 1,000 e-trucks is a threshold-and-deadline design worth comparing with city time-window regulations.
💡 Principal-agent lens: how should a shipper share EV costs and risk with carriers who own the assets?
💡 Joint siting of charging and routing on NH48: when does corridor electrification work at a 2,800-km scale?
Data leadsIndia's VAHAN vehicle registration dashboard for e-truck registrations by state and month, plus Amazon sustainability reports and CDP responses for fleet emissions.
Keywordsfleet turnover · emerging-market ev adoption · charging infrastructure · middle-mile logistics
Related research
Accelerating vehicle fleet turnover to achieve sustainable mobility goals (Journal of Operations Management, 2022)
Studies policies to accelerate vehicle fleet turnover for emissions goals, relevant to carrier-owned truck replacement.
Thread to pull

Three of today's items turn on a gate that firms must pass to operate: ERCOT and TCEQ approvals for data centers, FCC-recognized labs for electronics, and EUDR traceability proofs for soymeal. In each, the regulator controls access to a node rather than a product, and the outcome depends on who can most cheaply prove compliance or bypass the gate, such as behind-the-meter power, labs in mutual-recognition partner economies, or low-risk origins like Argentina. Comparing these access gates may suggest a common framework for compliance-driven supplier switching and leakage.
