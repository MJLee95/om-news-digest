01 · Energy & AI Data Centers

Umatilla, Oregon declares fiscal emergency over Gov. Kotek's pause on data center permits

DatacenterDynamics · Oct 23 · Read · ☆ Star

The week of Oct 20, 2026, the Umatilla City Council voted 5 of 6 members present to declare a local state of emergency over "the threat of widespread financial loss." The trigger is Gov. Tina Kotek's October directive, which stops Oregon DEQ from issuing new air and water permits for new or expanding data centers until July 2027. The city estimates more than $5 billion in regional investment could be delayed or lost, along with about $6 million in direct city losses, up to $12 million in water infrastructure investments and community-benefit payments, and at least 1,000 construction jobs.

BackgroundUmatilla is in eastern Oregon, not northwest Oregon as DCD reported. It has four operating data centers, one under construction and two planned, all from Amazon and Sabey Data Centers, with tax incentive agreements already signed. Umatilla County holds 22 of Oregon's more than 100 data centers (ECOnorthwest). DCD calls the measure a 'state-wide ban,' but it is a temporary pause on DEQ permits. Kotek is expected to propose data center reforms in the 2027 legislative session, which starts in late January.
ProblemThe state frames the pause as protecting public health and natural resources. Host communities have built their budgets and water infrastructure around data center payments and say projects could slip by two years or more. The resolution asks Kotek to withdraw or change the directive and calls for an economic impact assessment. It also lets City Manager Dave Stockdale coordinate with other local governments on possible legal action. He questions whether the pause exceeds DEQ's authority, violates the Administrative Procedures Act, or infringes Home Rule.
Where & whyThe friction sits at the environmental permitting stage of capacity siting. Data center developers commit capital, and towns commit infrastructure, under long-lived incentive and community benefit agreements. A state agency can then halt the permitting gate unilaterally. Several features drive the conflict. The state bears environmental and resource externalities while localities capture the tax base. Regulatory uncertainty is high because the pause ends in July 2027 but legislative reform is pending. Local finances are concentrated in a few hyperscale tenants. All loss figures are city projections that have not been independently checked.
Stakeholders
City of Umatilla and other local governments — About $6M in direct revenue, up to $12M in infrastructure payments, and jobs. They are weighing litigation against the state.
Gov. Kotek and Oregon DEQ — Want time to protect public health and natural resources and to shape 2027 legislative reforms.
Amazon and Sabey Data Centers — Planned and expanding capacity faces delays of two or more years, despite signed tax incentive agreements.
Construction workforce and residents — At least 1,000 construction jobs and hundreds of permanent jobs, against local environmental and resource impacts.
Research hints
💡 Treat the pause as a dated policy shock. Do planned Oregon projects relocate to neighboring states (e.g., Washington, Idaho), get delayed, or get cancelled? A difference-in-differences design using announced-project trackers could test this.
💡 View the dispute as federal-state-local jurisdictional conflict over a gating permit, and compare it with other siting moratoria.
💡 Consider a real-options model of capacity timing when the regulatory end date is known but a legislative regime change is pending.
Data leadsEIA-861 utility sales for Umatilla-area utilities; Oregon DEQ permit records; county property tax and enterprise zone agreements; state PUC large-load filings; EIA-860 for new generation tied to data centers.
Keywordssiting moratorium · regulatory uncertainty · facility siting · data center electricity demand · federal-state jurisdiction
Related research

Empirically studies how firms adjust sourcing and location choices under domestic versus upstream policy uncertainty, a lens for where data center capacity goes when one state pauses permits.

02 · Trade & Industrial Policy

Trump opts for 180 days of trade talks instead of tariffs after wind turbine Section 232 probe

Supply Chain Dive · Oct 23 · Read · ☆ Star

On Oct 22, 2026, Trump signed a proclamation closing the Section 232 investigation into wind turbine imports that Commerce opened in August 2025. Rather than imposing new tariffs, it directs Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and USTR Jamieson Greer to open or continue talks with trading partners and to report progress within 180 days. Existing steel, copper and aluminum tariffs that hit some turbine parts stay in place.

BackgroundThe proclamation says the U.S. 'is reliant' on imported turbines and parts, and that those imports have grown over the past decade. It cites 'unique cybersecurity risks' from foreign-made components, singling out inverters because most are made abroad. GHY International's summary also lists radar interference. The order calls wind 'unreliable' and 'burdensome' on the grid. In July, a Section 232 probe into commercial aircraft ended the same way, with talks instead of tariffs.
ProblemDevelopers and turbine OEMs face an open-ended threat. Trump said other remedies may follow depending on the talks. It is unknown which countries will take part or what happens if talks fail. Firms must decide whether to pull forward imports, qualify domestic or alternative suppliers, or wait.
Where & whyThe uncertainty lands at the procurement and project-scheduling stage for wind projects. Turbines and inverters are long-lead, concentrated imports. Policy is deliberately left contingent on negotiations, with a 180-day clock. Overlapping metals tariffs already raise component costs. The national-security framing, through cybersecurity and radar concerns, adds possible non-tariff restrictions beyond price.
Stakeholders
U.S. wind developers — Project costs and timelines depend on future import treatment of turbines and parts.
Foreign turbine and inverter manufacturers — Access to the U.S. market hinges on negotiated outcomes.
Commerce and USTR — Must deliver negotiated concessions within 180 days or justify further remedies.
Grid operators and utilities — The pace of wind capacity additions affects resource adequacy planning.
Research hints
💡 Look for pre-buy behavior: do turbine and inverter imports spike during the 180-day window compared with the aircraft-probe precedent?
💡 Tariff threat versus realized tariff: model sourcing under a regime-switching tariff process, building on tariff-hedging supplier development models.
💡 Cybersecurity-based sourcing restrictions on inverters may matter more than tariffs. Which components face non-price barriers?
Data leadsUSITC DataWeb and Census USA Trade Online imports of wind turbines, nacelles, blades and inverters by HTS line and month; Federal Register proclamation text; EIA-860 planned wind additions and delays; LBNL interconnection queues.
Keywordspre-buy behavior · regulatory uncertainty · sourcing restrictions · cyber-physical risk · supplier diversification
Related research
Tariff Hedging with a New Supplier? An Analysis of Sourcing Strategies Under Competition (Production and Operations Management, 2024)
Models whether competing firms should develop new contract manufacturers to hedge uncertain tariffs, which is the decision wind OEMs and developers face now.
Shock and Awe: A Theoretical Framework and Data Sources for Studying the Impact of 2025 Tariffs on Global Supply Chains (Journal of Supply Chain Management, 2025)
Provides a framework and data sources for studying 2025 tariff shocks and tariff uncertainty in supply chains.
03 · Critical Minerals & Batteries

DOE designates helium a critical material amid Hormuz-driven shortage

CBS News · Oct 23 · Read · ☆ Star

Around Oct 23, 2026, the Energy Department named helium a critical material, opening more federal financing to helium producers. The announcement came days after a 60 Minutes segment on the shortage. Energy Secretary Chris Wright cited helium's essential role in semiconductors and advanced energy technologies. Helium is the 20th critical material; the previous addition was metallurgical coal in 2025.

BackgroundThe Strait of Hormuz closed in February, trapping about a third of the world's helium supply in the Persian Gulf. In March, Iranian strikes hit Qatar's Ras Laffan facility, which produces helium. Seven months later the shortage persists, even though the U.S. is the world's largest producer, and 60 Minutes reports spot prices have tripled. The Federal Register notice says helium cannot be replaced in semiconductor fabrication, natural gas leak testing, quantum computing, and medical and scientific uses. By law, a critical material must face high supply-disruption risk and be essential to energy technology.
ProblemSupply is geographically concentrated and has no substitutes in key uses. Large domestic production has still not shielded U.S. users from a global chokepoint shock. The designation adds financing, but the amount and timing are unspecified. CBS attributes the timing to its own report, which is unverified.
Where & whyThe disruption originates at a maritime chokepoint and at a single large production site. It spreads through a byproduct commodity whose supply responds weakly to price. Downstream users such as fabs, hospitals and pipeline operators cannot substitute away. Policy responds after the fact, through the critical-material list and financing, rather than through pre-positioned buffers.
Stakeholders
Semiconductor manufacturers — Fab operations depend on non-substitutable helium at tripled spot prices.
U.S. helium producers — Gain access to federal financing to expand output.
Medical and research users — Face shortages and rationing for MRI and scientific equipment.
Department of Energy — Must show the designation reduces supply risk for energy and tech supply chains.
Research hints
💡 Byproduct economics: how does supply respond to a price spike when helium is co-produced with natural gas or LNG? Compare with other byproduct critical materials.
💡 Use the designation date as an event: do helium-dependent firms change their disclosures or inventory policies?
💡 Use a critical-node or chokepoint network lens to explain why domestic production share understates exposure.
Data leadsUSGS Mineral Commodity Summaries for helium production and prices; UN Comtrade and Census helium trade flows (Qatar vs. U.S. exports); Federal Register critical materials notice; SEC 10-K risk factors mentioning helium.
Keywordschokepoint disruption · raw material security · industrial policy · supplier concentration
Related research
Theorizing Critical Locus of Supply: A Material Flow Perspective (Journal of Supply Chain Management, 2025)
Introduces the critical locus of supply measure from a material-flow perspective, well suited to explaining how Hormuz and Ras Laffan dominate helium supply risk.
Perspectives on risk management in supply chains (Journal of Operations Management, 2009)
Foundational overview of supply chain risk management, useful framing for shortage mitigation by helium-dependent users.
04 · Food & Agriculture

Citizen scientists map North Carolina's confidential industrial poultry farm locations

Inside Climate News · Oct 24 · Read · ☆ Star

Three nonprofits — CleanAIRE Carolina, the Rural Empowerment Association for Community Help, and the N.C. Environmental Justice Network — launched the 'E-I-E-I-O No!' project on Zooniverse on July 28, 2026. More than 1,600 volunteers identified farms on NC One Map satellite imagery, and the results are being checked by in-person site visits. So far, six counties have yielded 2,011 hog farms and 2,349 poultry farms.

BackgroundWith some exceptions, North Carolina law lets poultry CAFOs operate without a permit, and their locations are confidential, known only to the N.C. Department of Agriculture. Some farms have as many as 48 barns. In December 2023, someone complained to the EPA's National Response Center about a stench bad enough to cause vomiting and blamed Smithfield's Tarheel hog slaughterhouse. The state Division of Air Quality found the real source was poultry manure spread on fields across Bladen County. NC State's Cooper Participatory Sciences Lab is an academic partner.
ProblemWithout location data, regulators and communities cannot attribute pollution or odor to sources, as the misattributed complaint shows. Researchers also cannot link farm density to environmental or health outcomes. The project fills this gap from outside the state system. Whether the full map will be published, and how industry and the state respond, could not be confirmed.
Where & whyThe gap sits at the farm level of integrated meat supply chains. Upstream growers supply processors, but the law exempts poultry CAFOs from permits and shields their locations. This information asymmetry favors producers and integrators over neighbors and regulators. Manure is applied to land far from barns, which spreads impacts and makes attribution harder.
Stakeholders
Environmental justice nonprofits and residents — Want visibility of pollution sources and accountability for air and water impacts.
Poultry growers and integrators — Their confidentiality protections and limited permitting burden could erode under public scrutiny.
N.C. Department of Agriculture and Division of Air Quality — Hold the only official location data and face pressure over enforcement.
Processors such as Smithfield — Risk being blamed for pollution from nearby operations, and face chain-liability exposure.
Research hints
💡 Treat the crowdsourced map as a new disclosure shock. Does public mapping change complaints, enforcement, or farm expansion?
💡 Chain liability: would processors' reputational exposure grow once supplier farm locations become public?
💡 Compare with hog CAFOs, which are permitted, to examine how disclosure regimes differ within one state.
Data leadsE-I-E-I-O No! Zooniverse farm map; EPA National Response Center complaint records; USDA NASS county poultry inventories; NC One Map imagery; NC DEQ air and water monitoring.
Keywordsvoluntary disclosure · multi-tier traceability · state environmental policy · regulatory enforcement
Related research
Chain liability in multitier supply chains? Responsibility attributions for unsustainable supplier behavior (Journal of Operations Management, 2014)
Shows consumers hold buying firms responsible for unsustainable upstream suppliers, relevant if mapped grower farms are linked to integrators.
Switching the Telescope Lens: A Sociomaterial Perspective of Sustainable Agricultural (Proto)Practices Transfer in an Agrifood Supply Chain (Journal of Operations Management, 2025)
A case study of sustainable practice transfer in a multi-tier poultry supply chain, offering a contrasting lens on grower-level practices.
05 · Logistics Decarbonization

Port of Los Angeles reports record-low emissions per container as volumes near 10.2M TEUs

Breakbulk.News · Oct 23 · Read · ☆ Star

The port's 2025 Inventory of Air Emissions, released Oct 22, 2026, shows its lowest emissions per container on record while it handled nearly 10.2 million TEUs. From 2024 to 2025, diesel particulate matter fell 2%, NOx 4% (the lowest since tracking began in 2005), sulfur oxides 5% and greenhouse gases 3%. Since 2005, diesel particulate matter is down 90%, NOx 72%, sulfur oxides 98% and greenhouse gases only 16%, while container volumes rose 37%.

Background2026 marks 20 years of the ports' Clean Air Action Plan. In 2025, truck diesel particulates fell 16% and truck NOx 25%. Electric cargo-handling equipment increased 20%, and zero-emission units now make up 14% of that fleet. 55% of harbor craft met Tier 3 or Tier 4 standards, up from 20% in 2017. Executive Director Gene Seroka said the port can 'move high cargo volumes while continuing to reduce emissions.'
ProblemCriteria pollutants have fallen steeply, but greenhouse gases are down only 16% since 2005. Engine standards and fuel rules cut local pollutants without deeply decarbonizing. Zero-emission equipment is still only 14% of the cargo-handling fleet. All figures are self-reported by the port.
Where & whyProgress comes from regulated, turnover-driven segments such as trucks, harbor craft, and fuel sulfur. Deep GHG cuts require electrifying capital-intensive, long-lived equipment and vessels, much of it owned by terminal operators, carriers and drayage firms rather than the port. This split creates misaligned incentives and slow fleet turnover.
Stakeholders
Port of Los Angeles — Wants to show growth and emissions cuts are compatible and to meet Clean Air Action Plan goals.
Terminal operators and drayage truckers — Bear the capital costs of electric equipment and newer trucks.
Ocean carriers and harbor craft operators — Face pressure to upgrade engines and fuels.
Port-adjacent communities — Experience the health effects of diesel PM and NOx.
Research hints
💡 Decompose the emissions change into scale, mix and technique effects by source category; the GHG versus NOx gap is the puzzle.
💡 Fleet turnover models: what drives the adoption rate of zero-emission cargo-handling equipment among terminal operators?
💡 Compare the Port of Long Beach's inventory, or ports without similar programs, to estimate the effect of the Clean Air Action Plan.
Data leadsPort of LA annual Inventories of Air Emissions (2005–2025); NOAA MarineCadastre AIS vessel tracks for at-berth and transit activity; CARB drayage truck registry; Port of Long Beach emissions inventories for comparison.
Keywordsfleet turnover · emissions standards · maritime policy · emissions forecasting
Related research
Accelerating vehicle fleet turnover to achieve sustainable mobility goals (Journal of Operations Management, 2022)
Studies how to accelerate vehicle fleet turnover for climate goals, the binding constraint behind the port's slow GHG decline.
Shipping Emission Control Area Optimization Considering Carbon Emission Reduction (Operations Research, 2024)
Optimizes shipping emission control areas with carbon in mind, relevant to why sulfur rules cut SOx far more than GHG.
Thread to pull

Three of today's items show policy left deliberately contingent: Oregon's permit pause ending in July 2027 ahead of 2027 legislative reform, the 180-day wind turbine talks, and helium financing of unspecified size. In each, firms must commit long-lived capacity or inventory before the rules settle. Comparing how developers, OEMs and fabs time investments under these 'pending regime' shocks, perhaps with a common real-options or pre-buy lens, could reveal whether known deadlines produce bunching that permanent uncertainty does not.

New in the journals

Bargaining and Contract Forms in Distribution Channels: Why Do Slotting Fees Arise and Linear Contracts Persist?
Management Science

The paper asks why simple linear contracts persist in grocery channels and why fixed payments usually flow from suppliers to retailers as slotting fees. A multilateral bargaining model, in which a supplier negotiates separately with competing retailers, generates both patterns through a trade-off in contract choice.

Bargaining power shapes who pays fixed fees, which is useful for thinking about community benefit agreements between data center developers and host towns like Umatilla.
