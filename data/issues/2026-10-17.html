01 · Energy & AI Data Centers

PJM ran on 66% of its generation fleet during October Maximum Generation Alerts; about a quarter of outages were unplanned

PA Environment Digest Blog · Oct 16 · Read · ☆ Star

During the week of Oct. 12, 2026, PJM had just over 61,000 MW of generation out (only 66% of fleet capacity available). More than two-thirds of outages were planned maintenance and about 15,000 MW were unplanned (preliminary), roughly a quarter rather than the one-third in some headlines. PJM recalled about 10,000 MW and called Demand Response on Oct. 13–14 for weather it called warm but 'not extreme.' DR has now been used on 15 days in 2025–2026 versus 8 in all of 2015–2024. PJM blames year-round data center load growing faster than supply and transmission, plus longer weather extremes that push into spring and fall maintenance seasons.

OM anglePlanned-maintenance scheduling is a classic OM problem, and data center load is flattening the seasonal demand trough it depends on. Outage coordination, recall decisions and DR use now trade reliability against winter readiness.
Research QHow does growth in year-round large-load demand change the optimal timing of generator maintenance, and has it raised shoulder-season scarcity events and prices in PJM?
Data / IDUse PJM Data Miner for hourly load, outages, emergency actions and DR events from 2015–2026. Combine with EIA-860 and EIA-923 for unit characteristics and EIA-930 for hourly demand. Exploit zone-level variation in data center load growth (e.g., Dominion zone vs. others) in a panel of zone-days to test whether shoulder-season alerts, real-time prices and maintenance deferrals rise with large-load share.
Keywordsdata center electricity demand · grid reliability standards · maintenance scheduling · demand response · load forecasting
Related research
Dynamic Pricing of Peak Production (Operations Research, 2015)
Models how peak producers price scarce flexible capacity in transient high-demand periods, relevant to the scarcity prices and peaking economics behind PJM's alerts.
02 · Trade & Industrial Policy

FMC proposes $200,000 per-voyage fee on Spanish-flagged vessels and possible limits on Spanish imports

Supply Chain Dive · Oct 16 · Read · ☆ Star

On Oct. 14 the Federal Maritime Commission proposed a $200,000 per-voyage fee on Spanish-flagged vessels and possible import restrictions on Spanish perfumery and cosmetics, oils and fats, ceramics, stone articles, and sugar and confectionery (docket FMC-2026-0463). The action follows a 2024 investigation finding Spain denied port access to U.S.-flagged ships, including two Maersk vessels carrying Maritime Security Program cargo; Spain cited EU regulations. Comments are due Dec. 14, Spain will be offered a hearing, and the docket expects restricted goods to be replaced mostly from other countries.

OM angleThis is a narrow, product-specific trade shock with a clear announcement date. It allows study of importer sourcing substitution and of carrier flag or routing responses.
Research QFollowing the FMC announcement, how fast do U.S. importers of the targeted Spanish product categories shift to alternative origins, and does pre-buying occur before restrictions take effect?
Data / IDUse USITC DataWeb or Census USA Trade Online monthly imports by HS line. Compare targeted Spanish categories to untreated Spanish goods and to the same HS lines from Italy, Portugal and France in a difference-in-differences design around Oct. 14 and the final rule date. Panjiva bill-of-lading data (commercial) can add importer-level substitution, and the Federal Register docket supplies event timing.
Keywordssourcing restrictions · supplier diversification · pre-buy behavior · maritime policy
Related research
Interplay between Competing and Coexisting Policy Regimens within Supply Chain Configurations (Production and Operations Management, 2021)
Studies how conflicting policy regimes across jurisdictions shape supply chain configurations, which parallels the clash between EU rules Spain cited and U.S. maritime retaliation.
03 · Sustainable Operations

Haleon adds an EU ETS carbon price to supplier bids, shifting 1–2 of every 10 deals

Trellis · Oct 15 · Read · ☆ Star

Haleon asks most suppliers to estimate the carbon footprint of each purchase. It multiplies that figure by the EU ETS price (about €80/t CO2) and adds it to the bid, so the lowest combined total wins. The charge averages about 2% of bid value (about 10% for plastic bottles, about 1% for acetaminophen) and shifts 1–2 in 10 deals to pricier, lower-carbon bidders, per Haleon's Chris Low. Nearly a quarter of key suppliers now measure emissions directly, up from none in 2024. Haleon is piloting contracts with annual emission-reduction commitments and financial penalties, toward a 42% Scope 3 cut over 2022–2030; its latest report shows 13%.

OM angleA shadow carbon price in scoring auctions is a concrete mechanism-design lever for Scope 3. It raises questions about supplier misreporting of footprints and the incentive to invest in measurement.
Research QIn a scoring auction that adds a carbon charge based on supplier-reported footprints, when does the buyer gain from rewarding primary measurement over industry-average estimates, and how much emission reduction does the charge buy per dollar of higher procurement cost?
Data / IDAnalytical: model a scoring auction with a carbon price and private, possibly misreported, footprints. Empirical: use the CDP supply chain module, where Haleon's suppliers respond, to compare supplier measurement adoption and emissions before and after 2024 against suppliers of peer consumer-health firms. SBTi dashboard target validations can serve as a supplier-level outcome.
Keywordssupplier emissions · scope 3 disclosure · internal carbon pricing · procurement auctions
Related research
Helping hampered bidders—Do subsidy auctions work as intended? (Journal of Operations Management, 2024)
Empirically evaluates preferential (subsidy) procurement auctions, a close analogue to Haleon tilting bid scoring toward low-carbon suppliers.
Customer base environmental disclosure and supplier greenhouse gas emissions: A signaling theory perspective (Journal of Operations Management, 2023)
Examines whether customers' environmental disclosure lowers supplier GHG emissions, the downstream outcome Haleon's mechanism targets.
04 · Circular Economy

Patagonia folds Worn Wear resale into its main store after selling ~160,000 used items ($13M) in fiscal 2026

Trellis · Oct 16 · Read · ☆ Star

Patagonia is closing the separate nine-year-old Worn Wear site and listing used items next to new ones on its main store, with a shared cart and the same warranty. It sold almost 160,000 verified secondhand items (about $13M) in fiscal 2026. More than 40 staff in Reno processed about 230,000 garments in 2025, and about 80% were resellable. The switch took a multiyear IT overhaul to handle unique SKUs, discontinued items and condition-based pricing that Trove previously managed.

OM angleMixing one-of-a-kind used inventory with new stock raises cannibalization, uncertain-supply and condition-pricing questions that closed-loop supply chain models have mostly studied in separate channels.
Research QDoes listing used items alongside new ones on a brand's main site cannibalize or complement new-product sales, and how should condition-based prices be set when returns supply is uncertain?
Data / IDEvent study around the integration date using web-scraped listings and prices from Patagonia's site, comparing product categories with high versus low used availability. Brands that keep resale separate, such as Eileen Fisher Renew, serve as a comparison. Analytically, model joint pricing of new and graded used units with stochastic take-back supply.
Keywordsclosed-loop supply chain · recommerce · cannibalization · condition-based pricing
Related research
The Future of Retail Operations (Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 2019)
Reviews retail operations research and open directions, a useful map for positioning omnichannel resale integration.
05 · Supply Chain Resilience

Hormuz attacks hit a record six ships in one day; Singapore-flagged feeder GFS Pride sinks with containers aboard

FreightWaves · Oct 16 · Read · ☆ Star

At least 10 vessels were attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz from Oct. 11, including six on that day alone, the most in a single day since the U.S.–Iran war began in February. UKMTO logged 12 attacks Oct. 5–11, a weekly record. The 2,824-TEU feeder GFS Pride was struck Oct. 13 and sank off Oman with all 21 crew safe; the cause is disputed between a mine and projectiles or a drone. GFS Giselle and DP World Kaveri were also hit, and Maersk, ONE, PIL and OOCL had schedules on some affected ships. Kpler counted 131 transits in the week to Oct. 14 versus 176 the week before, with crude flows down to about 12.6M b/d from about 15M at end-September.

OM angleSlot-sharing on feeder ships spreads risk to carriers whose names appear on bills of lading. War-risk insurance can be cancelled on short notice in these cases, a contractual fragility in shared transport capacity.
Research QHow do carriers adjust capacity sharing (slot charters on third-party feeders) and freight rates in response to escalating chokepoint attack intensity?
Data / IDBuild a weekly panel from UKMTO incident counts and IMO casualty data as the shock measure. Outcomes are Kpler or AIS-based transit counts and Xeneta or Drewry rates on Gulf lanes (commercial; public Drewry WCI headlines as a fallback). GDELT event counts can serve as a public intensity proxy. Compare Gulf feeder services to non-Gulf services operated by the same carriers.
Keywordsfreight rates · chokepoint disruption · war-risk insurance · slot sharing
Thread to pull

Haleon's carbon-adjusted bids and PJM's capacity stress share one feature: a shadow price on a scarce resource (carbon, or peak capacity) changes which supplier or unit wins. A study could compare how often a carbon adder versus a reliability or scarcity adder flips procurement decisions, for example for data centers procuring power. Analytically, this could be a scoring-auction model that prices both emissions and delivery risk.

M&SOM

This survey organizes OM research on the sharing economy into eight categories (ride, space, bike and vehicle sharing, group buying, crowdfunding and others). For each it outlines advances and open problems around matching, pricing and capacity.

Carrier slot-sharing on Hormuz feeders and Patagonia's peer-sourced used inventory are both shared-capacity models that this framework helps analyze.

The Buy-in Effect: When Increasing Initial Effort Encourages Follow-Through
Management Science

In a field experiment with a state transportation department, adding modest friction to carpool-platform sign-up cut sign-ups 25% but increased overall usage. Selection explains only part of the effect.

It suggests that requiring effortful steps, as Haleon does by asking suppliers to measure emissions directly, could raise follow-through on sustainability programs.
